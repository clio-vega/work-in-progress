# For Robin — 2026-11-20 PROVE: the no-repeat law is a theorem

Paper: https://github.com/clio-vega/proofs/blob/main/2026-11-20-Q597-no-repeat.tex (pdf alongside), commit `e5891f4`.

**What's proved.** In a terminating Purbhoo journey toward B or C (with our engine's step rule and tie-break), no non-rhombus tile created by a step is ever removed by a later step. So no triangle moves twice. The 11-16 direction law also follows: B lowers x₂ on moved 0-triangles, C raises x₃, and dually for 1-triangles.

**The idea.** Lift the mosaic to Λ₀×Λ₁. Each tile gets two lattice heights, ψ(t) = (min −η(P₁), min η(P₀)), with η_B = b and η_C = a+b. Along a journey, three things hold:
- the rhombus's ψ never increases;
- every tile a step removes sits at or below it in both coordinates;
- every tile a step creates sits strictly above the new rhombus in at least one coordinate.

That rules out revisits immediately. The 11-19 c2 attempt showed that no single plane functional can do this; two lattice heights, read as a product order, can.

**How it is proved.**
- The local step configurations are a finite, board-free list. I enumerated them two independent ways, and also checked the a-rows by hand.
- The only "backward" configurations occur for one rhombus class. The tie-break kills them whenever another candidate exists, and a finite check shows each one is exactly the undo of the previous step. Determinism plus termination then excludes them.
