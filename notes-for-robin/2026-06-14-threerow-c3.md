# c=3 family: the first real |J*|=4 (two-generator box) — 2026-06-14

Robin —

Today's prove session cracked the **structure** of the `c=3` family `(a,b,3)` — the first place the
full two-generator box `{0,2,4,6}` appears (`|J*|=4`, minimal witness `(9,6,3)=3·(3,2,1)`). I didn't
fully *close* it, but I got all the load-bearing machinery and reduced what's left to two explicit,
verified inequalities. Here's the honest state.

## What's proved (no m-cap)
1. **Closed form** for `M_j`: `C(N,b−j)(a−b+1)Q₃ / [6(a+4−j)(b+1−j)(b+2−j)(b+3−j)]`, sextic `Q₃`.
   Verified vs Murnaghan–Nakayama, m≤24, 0 mismatch. (The `c`-grading of the denominator and the
   `(a−b+1)` prefactor is now unmistakable: c! · (a+c+1−j) · ∏(b+i−j).)
2. **The two-generator identity** `Q₃ = (a−1)(b−2)·H − 6!·C(j,6)`. This is the heart. The tip
   `−6!C(j,6)` (sixfold root, first nonzero 720 at j=6) is the c=3 analogue of c2's `+4!C(j,4)`.
3. **Prop-2** Kummer: literally the c2 formula with `(a+3,b+2)→(a+4,b+3)`.
4. **Offset theorem** — fully proved. The offset `j₀ = min J*` is `0` if `a` even, `3` if `a` odd
   (a forced descent `Δ(1)=−1, Δ(2)=−2, Δ(3)=−3`, proved from the identity). *Same law as c=1.*

## The picture (verified m≤39)
- `a` even: `J*={0,2}` (a≡2,b≡3) or `{0,4}` (a≡0,b≡1) or `{0}` — generators **independent**, never
  both, `|J*|≤2`. Structurally a clone of c2.
- `a` odd: `J*=3+{0,2,4,6}` (a≡1,b≡2) or `{3}` — generators **locked together**, `|J*|∈{1,4}`.
  The lock is because the heavy factor `(a−1)(b−2)` is divisible by 4 exactly in the a-odd branch,
  which pays *both* the v₂(j)=1 and v₂(j)=2 deficits at the shifted offset simultaneously.

So the general law is now legible: **`c` sets the tip order `(2c)!C(j,2c)`** (generators 2,4,…,2c),
**`parity(a)` sets the offset**, and generator *coexistence* is a heavy-factor divisibility event.

## The two gaps (each verified m≤79, neither hand-proved)
- **(L3″-A)**, a even: `v₂C(b+3,j)+v₂Q₃(j) ≥ v₂(b+3)+1`. The naive `v₂Q₃≥v₂(j)+1` fails at j=4 —
  the binomial slack must pay, exactly the c2 Number-Lemma phenomenon but with an *odd* heavy
  factor (so the c2 subset-identity trick doesn't port verbatim).
- **Compensation Lemma B**, a odd: `Δ̃(j)=val(j)−val(3) ≥ 0` for j≥4, with the all-or-nothing tie on
  {5,7,9}. This is the genuine **two-generator Number Lemma** — it must produce two simultaneous
  deficits keyed on the single switch a≡1,b≡2. This is the real prize and where I'd point a LEAN or
  a fresh prove session next.
- Plus the usual boundary residual (m≤79).

I think (L3″-A) is the easier of the two and probably falls to a careful `C(j,6)`-tip subset
identity; Lemma B is the one that, once proved, likely generalises to all `c`. Both are queued.
