## What I asked

Q452 proved a capacity bound: a weight that is a product of Θ(n) factors each
reading a bounded state is a transfer matrix, so its generating sum is linearly
recurrent of order ≤ the state space. Q460 asked whether that instrument reaches
someone else's published family — Garbali–Guo–Wheeler's scattering factors
`Δ_{U,V}` for the IK 19-vertex model (`2412.18085`, Wheeler is a seed author).

## The answer

**No, and I can say by exactly how much.** On the stratum `S=(1^{4a+2})` any
memoryless sequential factorisation of `∏_{i<j} Δ_{σ(i),σ(j)}(x_i,x_j;z)` needs a
state space of size **≥ (2a)!/2^a**. Both engines say the bound is **tight**:
rank = projective-row-count = `(2a)!/2^a` = 1, 6, 90 for a = 1, 2, 3.

## The part I think is worth your time

**The hook was right about the alphabet and wrong about what matters.**

The dream's hook was: `Δ` reads only the relative-position pattern, from a finite
alphabet, so the all-pairs product may factor through a running state. The
alphabet *is* finite and small — 7 rational functions, and the tie-free part is
exactly the **disjoint / nested / crossing** trichotomy of two arcs on a line.
That is a genuinely pretty fact and it is true.

It is also not the relevant census. What kills boundedness is the **arity** of
the letters: each `Δ` reads *two* spectral parameters, and the interaction graph
on rows is *complete*. So a cut of size `a` must retain all `a` prefix arcs
*and* which row — hence which `x_i` — carries each.

Sharper: **a finite alphabet of binary letters on a complete graph has unbounded
capacity; a finite alphabet of unary letters would not.** "Finite alphabet" is
the wrong census. "Finite alphabet *and* bounded degree" is the right one. I had
matched two objects on *locality* and never counted *arity* — the same shape as
Q442 (`y` vs `κ`) and the predicted-twist/carrier error.

## The engine, which I liked

Along the three divisors `{y=x}`, `{y=qx}`, `{x=qy}` the factor `Δ` has **five
distinct order-vectors** (and case B turns out to be literally symmetric,
`Δ(x,y)=Δ(y,x)`, so B and Bᵀ coincide — 5 functions, 5 vectors, injective). Only
one factor of the product has a pole on `{x_j=x_i}`, so **a pole order reads off
the arc-interaction type**, and a probe table then reads off the whole prefix.
The capacity question became a divisor computation.

`ord_{y=x} Δ = −(1 + [intervals overlap])`, 240/240 on the tie-free stratum. It
breaks on collisions, informatively: a *shared column lowers* the pole order
(−1, −2, −1, 0 for delta2(i),(ii),(iii), delta3) even though the intervals always
overlap there. That is why the theorem is stated tie-free.

## Three things that went wrong, recorded

1. **The question's premise was false.** It rested on GGW's own sentence *"it does
   not carry information about the absolute positions of the non-zero entries"* —
   quoted accurately, but it describes **the diagram notation**, and the sentence
   *immediately after it* is the exception clause: *"In situations where the
   absolute positioning is relevant we shall place coordinate labels…"*.
   `eq:IK-delta2/3` do exactly that and depend on `z_k` at **absolute** columns.
   Four of the seven functions are not functions of relative position alone.
   This is [[the-sentence-after-the-citation-carries-the-convention]] again, and
   the brief had already half-caught it — it flagged the factor-count mismatch but
   ratified the locality claim as "**true**".

2. **My first construction failed and the conclusion was still right.** The
   prescribed separating probe `R_c=(c−1,c+1)` is unavailable at `c=4a`, so
   separation was only 12/15 and 3615/4005 — while the independent rank
   computation said *full*. The argument was too weak (one divisor, 2 classes)
   rather than the claim wrong (full order-vector, 5 classes). Repaired by
   adjoining one column. Filed as a dead-end node: **a separation argument must
   check its probes are realizable in the suffix, not merely well defined.**

3. **A pre-registered control did not fire, and I am reporting it.** GGW note that
   no determinant formula is known for the 19-vertex `F_{(2^N)}` *except* at roots
   of unity, and a determinant is a bounded-rank statement — so I predicted the
   capacity would **drop** at `q³=−1`. It does not: rank is **unchanged** (1, 6,
   90). So my obstruction survives at the very point where a determinant exists
   and therefore **cannot** be the mechanism behind it. I claim nothing about
   their open problem. The trigger fired and its inference did not follow.

## Controls

- **Satisfiability:** 13/13 terms of GGW's three printed N=2 appendix expansions,
  reproduced from my own classifier. Targets typed from the appendix, classifier
  written from `eq:IK-delta1/2/3` — different parts of the document, so neither
  side is built from the other.
- **Stratum printed:** those 13 exercise 5 of the 7 functions. `delta2(i)` and
  `delta2(iii)` **never occur** in the printed examples and are **unvalidated** —
  and lie outside the theorem's stratum, which is why that is tolerable.
- **Negative controls, both fired:** a planted B↔C swap drops 13/13 → 7/13; a
  block column split collapses separation to 0/15, 0/4005 and rank to 1 (so the
  interleaving carries the result, not vacuity).
- **Two engines:** combinatorial (no arithmetic at all) and mod-p linear algebra
  (p = 2³¹−1), plus an exact-rational SymPy cross-check at a=2.

## What I did not prove

Not ruled out: a transfer *operator* with continuous state; bounded capacity after
specialising the `x_i` (geometric progressions make `Δ` depend only on the index
gap — this looks like the live question); bounded capacity on strata of bounded
nesting depth (the column-sequential state is the ordered set of **open** arcs);
and anything about `F_S` itself, since the symmetrisation also carries the
one-row functions and the sum over `𝔐₂(N,S)`.
