## Result

Gap 3 of yesterday's paper — a closed form for $[R_e(t),R_{e'}(t)]/(1+t)$ — is closed, and so is
the sharpness half the brief flagged as "the one that should close".

The move: stop working with skew shapes. On the Maya diagram, adding an $e$-ribbon is a bead hop
$b\mapsto b+e$ weighted $t^{\#\text{beads jumped}}$, so the commutator is a signed sum over
**two-hop paths** — and there are exactly three kinds of path (same bead twice; second bead lands
on the vacated site; two independent beads). Classifying them gives every entry:

- $\mu/\lambda$ an $(e+e')$-ribbon of height $h$: entry $(\epsilon_e-\epsilon_{e'})t^{h-1}(1+t)$,
  where $\epsilon_j=1$ iff the ribbon turns upward at cut point $j$.
- two beads: $\pm t^{H-1}(1-t^2)$ iff the two hops **cross**, else $0$.
- otherwise $0$.

So every entry of $Q=[R_e,R_{e'}]/(1+t)$ is $0$, $\pm t^a$ or $\pm t^a(1-t)$. Sharpness: two
witness families uniform in $2\le e<e'$, $\varnothing\to(e',1^e)$ with entry $t^{e-1}(1+t)$ and
$\varnothing\to(e',e)$ with entry $1-t^2$, force $\gcd=t+1$ **exactly**; and in fact *every*
nonzero entry carries $(1+t)$ exactly once.

**The corollary I did not expect.** A hop $b\mapsto b+e$ makes a ribbon occupying exactly the
diagonals $(b,b+e]$, so: *two ribbon additions commute as graded operators unless their intervals
of diagonals properly cross.* Disjoint intervals commute — of course. **Nested intervals also
commute.** A small ribbon inside the diagonal span of a large one is invisible to it, and no
parity or degree argument would have predicted that. At $e=e'$ it specialises to the $|b-c|\ge e$
criterion answered on MO 292312 in 2018 and never cited.

238594/238594 entries against brute force, $|\lambda|\le8$, all $2\le e<e'\le7$. Six controls,
variation named before each; all fire. The load-bearing one: perturbing $h\to h+1$ **breaks** the
formula (761/6665), so the check is not blind to the grading.
