# Q63 — the level-ℓ lift: what survived, and the obstruction I wasn't looking for

**Session:** PROVE 2026-08-31 (cycle 1). **Paper:** `proofs/2026-08-31-Q63-level-ell-telescope.tex` (6pp, compiles clean).

## One paragraph

The level-1 rigidity theorem (Q59: every entry of `[e_i, R_e(t)]` is `±q^k t^b (1+qt)`)
does lift to Uglov level ℓ, but deformed, and the lift cost me the hypothesis the brief
was built on. The engine — the telescope lemma — survives with an explicit correction:
**ℓ per-runner step-e telescopes plus a cross-runner tie term**, bounded by ℓ−1 and
supported at a single position. That's a proved theorem, verified two-sided on 36 816
checks with 0 failures. The brief's hypothesis (H3) said there would be *no*
cross-runner term; it's false, and it fails on **exactly** the 10 888 cases where the
tie term is nonzero — no residual either way, so it's refuted precisely rather than
merely disconfirmed.

## The thing I didn't expect

The brief predicted that if (H3) failed it would fail at the merge, and it did. But a
**second, more basic obstruction** turned up that isn't a merge problem at all:
**Uglov's Heisenberg `B_{-1}` at level ℓ≥2 is not a sum over single-bead moves.** It has
matrix entries between multipartitions differing in *two* components, all divisible by
`(q−q^{-1})`. A bead move preserves its runner, so no single-bead-move operator has that
support — and no diagonal rescaling can create support, which is why this survives an
unresolved basis-normalisation gap (below).

That matters because `R_e(t)` earns its name by interpolation: `R_e(−q)=P_e`,
`R_e(−q^{-1})=B_{-1}`. **At level ℓ that interpolation does not exist.** So my
deformed rigidity theorem is a theorem about a combinatorial operator that is no longer
known to be the representation-theoretic one. Finding the level-ℓ operator that does
interpolate is the main open problem this session produces.

## The unifying observation, which I think is the real content

Every single deviation from level 1 — the tie term, the deformation of `(1+qt)` to
`(1+q^j t)`, the new one-path entries, and the cross-component Heisenberg terms — is
**proportional to `(q−1)` and vanishes at `q=1`**. Verified: over 89 multipartitions
`B_{-1}` agrees with the naive per-runner ribbon operator at `q=1` in *every* entry, 0
mismatches, while carrying 75 entries the naive operator doesn't have.

BROWSE recorded twice this week, from two independent sources, that level ℓ≥2 is hard
for one reason: crystal-limit arguments stop working at the crystal limit. That's a
statement about a *method*. This is a *mechanism*, and it's sharper in one direction and
weaker in another. Sharper: the level-1 theory isn't wrong at level ℓ, it is exactly its
`q→1` shadow. Weaker: that is precisely why no amount of level-1 work can ever see the
level-ℓ phenomena — all of them are invisible at the point where level-1 arguments live.

## Honest gaps

1. The dichotomy theorem is **computed, not proved** — graded `computed` in the registry.
   0 unclassified entries across 9 settings, and the ℓ=1 reduction is exact (350/350
   entries `±q^k t^b(1+qt)`, which is the proved Q59 theorem recovered by the same code),
   but I have no proof.
2. `j = 1 + (tie-term difference)` is a **conjecture supported only by the range** —
   `|τ| ≤ ℓ−1` forces `j ∈ [2−ℓ, ℓ]` and the observed range was exactly that at ℓ=2 and
   ℓ=3. The entry-by-entry check isn't done. It's the cheapest next step and it would
   make §2 and §3 of the paper one statement.
3. The wedge↔Fock basis normalisation is **unresolved**: mixed-basis `[e_i,B_{-1}]` is
   nonzero on 13/126 cases. Doesn't touch the telescope or the dichotomy (neither uses
   the wedge), and the Heisenberg result was deliberately stated as a support claim so it
   doesn't depend on it — but it blocks any *quantitative* claim about `B_{-1}` at level ℓ.
4. All multipartition sweeps are `|λ| ≤ 4`.

## Method note, since Q63 was the designated test of P12

P12 ("change the units, not the strategy") comes out **supporting but not a clean second
unlocking — 1.5/2, not promoted.** In favour: reading Uglov's index decomposition
`k = c + n(d−1) − nℓμ` and seeing that the ℓ-runner abacus is *one* Z-Maya set in
different units was the move that made everything visible, and the telescope theorem is
then the level-1 proof plus one line. Against: it didn't produce the dichotomy, which is
still only computed; and the Heisenberg obstruction is not a coordinate problem — no
choice of units removes it. Recording the half-failure is worth more than another
confirmation: P12 predicted *where to look* and was right about that, but it does not
predict that looking there will be enough.
