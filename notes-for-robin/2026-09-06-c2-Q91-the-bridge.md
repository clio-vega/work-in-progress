## The headline, which is not the one I expected

I proved the conjecture. Then I found out the operator is 22 years old.

**Theorem (new, I believe).** In the *undeformed* Clifford algebra,
$$R_e(t)\;=\;\sum_{b\in\mathbb Z}\psi_{b+e}\,\psi_b^{*}\,(-t)^{N_{(b,b+e)}},\qquad
  N_{(b,b+e)}=\textstyle\sum_{b<j<b+e} n_j .$$
The fermionic sign gives $(-1)^{\mathrm{ht}}$ for **free** — it is just what you pay to anticommute
a bead past the beads it jumps — and the diagonal dressing turns it into $t^{\mathrm{ht}}$. So the
whole $t$-dependence is diagonal, and $R_e(t)$ is a **finite** expression of fermionic degree
$\le 2e$, even though (yesterday's theorem) its differential order on $\Lambda$ is infinite for
$t\ne-1$. The infinitude was never a property of the operator; it is a property of the coordinates
$p_1,p_2,\dots$

**Corollary.** At $t=-1$ the scalar $(-t-1)$ vanishes, the dressing collapses to $1$, and
$R_e(-1)=\sum_b\psi_{b+e}\psi_b^*=M_{p_e}$. Yesterday's $0/\infty$ discontinuity of
$\operatorname{ord}R_e(c)$ at $c=-1$ is therefore *the vanishing of one scalar $1+t$* — not a second
phenomenon. The point the whole programme is anchored at is the point where the dressing is invisible.

**Theorem.** $[R_e(t)^{*},R_e(t)]=1+t^2+\cdots+t^{2(e-1)}$, by an explicit telescoping potential
$g(b)=\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}m(b+i)\,t^{2\#(M\cap(b+i,b+e))}$ on the Maya diagram.

## And then the literature

I designed that last computation as a **separator** — a coordinate with no parameter to fit — hoping
to *distinguish* my operator from the LLT/KMS bosons. It matched them exactly. Following it back:

Lam, *Ribbon Schur operators* (`math/0409463`), §2, defines
$u_i^{(n)}:\lambda\mapsto q^{\mathrm{spin}(\mu/\lambda)}\mu$ over $n$-ribbons with head on diagonal
$i$, with $\mathrm{spin}=(\text{rows})-1$ — **my height, exactly**. Summing over diagonals,
$\sum_i u_i^{(e)}|_{q=t}=R_e(t)$, and his Cor. `cor:KMS` identifies $\sum_i u_i=B_{-1}$: the
**Kashiwara–Miwa–Stern boson** on the level-1 $q$-deformed Fock space of
$U_q(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_e)$. My central term is his eq. `(hei)` at $k=1$. Rediscovered, not new.

This is a match of *definitions*, not of shapes, so there is nothing to argue about. **And it is the
thing I have been trying to find for weeks.** The ribbon path — Murnaghan–Nakayama, border strips,
heights — is now inside the $q$-Fock / integrable-lattice literature, where
$U_q(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_e)$, canonical bases and the vertex machinery are on the shelf. I'd
rather have the bridge than the novelty.

What still looks new is the **normal form** itself: nobody I can find writes this operator in the
undeformed Clifford algebra. Lam works combinatorially on partitions (his two papers contain no
occurrence of *fermion*, *Clifford*, $\psi$, *wedge*, *bead* — I grepped the sources); KMS and Uglov
work inside a **$q$-deformed** wedge. The content of my theorem is that the deformation can be moved
entirely out of the algebra and into a diagonal weight. **But that is an absence measured over my own
disk, and I have flagged it as such in the paper** — I have not read Bloch–Okounkov, and a
$W_{1+\infty}$ search is exactly where this could already exist under another name.

## Verification, and one methodological catch worth your time

Two code-disjoint engines agree 485/485 ($e\le6$, $|\lambda|\le9$, $t$ symbolic); a *third*
independent engine (monomial basis + Kostka, no MN, no abacus) confirms the $t=-1$ anchor 90/90.

The brief prescribed three planted-defect controls. **Two of them were the same control.** I only
found that by testing the controls *against each other*: a valid bead move forces $b\in M$ and
$b+e\notin M$, so **both endpoints of the interval carry no information** — "closed interval" and
"exponent $N{+}1$" are literally the same perturbation. Three further pairs collapsed too. I replaced
them with five genuinely distinct defects, all observed to fail, and recorded the kernel in the paper.
A control you have not checked for degeneracy is not a control.
