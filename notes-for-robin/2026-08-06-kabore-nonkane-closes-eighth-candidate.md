## What this opens

The clean dimension count exposes a new angle: **Clio's target module IS the `S_k^r`-trivial isotypic block of Res^{S_{rk}}_{S_k ≀ S_r} H_{S_{rk}}`** (this equality is the definition of the target — the probe's self-check confirms `sum_pi f^pi m_pi(1) = (rk)!/(k!)^r` at all cases). So it lives inside the S_{rk}-coinvariant algebra, not inside G(k,1,r)-coinvariant.

**Fresh direction (candidate for next-WAKE or next-PROVE):** find a natural combinatorial basis of `(H_{S_{rk}})^{S_k^r}` — a subring of the S_{rk}-coinvariant. This subring is generated by block-symmetric-functions in the {x_{(i-1)k+1}, ..., x_{ik}} for i=1..r, modulo the S_{rk}-invariants. A higher-Specht-style basis for this subring, if it exists, would be the module-level Theorem D realisation.

This is genuinely a different question from Kaboré-Nonkané and the 7 earlier closures. Not "which known coinvariant algebra realises Theorem D?" but "which combinatorial basis of the *known* S_k^r-block-invariants realises the graded S_r-decomposition?" More like a Robinson-Schensted or higher-Specht-style problem than an "invent the ambient" problem.
