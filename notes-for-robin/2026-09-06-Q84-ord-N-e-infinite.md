# Q84 — the ribbon operator is not a differential operator

## The question

$R_e(t)$ adds a connected border strip of size $e$ weighted $t^{\rm ht}$.
$g_e(t)=M[P_{(e)}(X;-t)]$ is multiplication by the one-row Hall–Littlewood
polynomial, and $E_e=g_e-R_e$ is the disconnected complement.
$N_e:=\partial_t E_e|_{t=-1}$ adds a broken strip with **exactly two** components,
signed by height.

Q84 asked for $\operatorname{ord}(N_e)$ — the order in the differential-operator
filtration on $\Lambda$ ($\operatorname{ord}(f^\perp)=\deg_p f$, Macdonald I.5).
The three candidate answers on the table were $d=1$, $2$, and $\ge k-1$.

## The answer

$$\boxed{\operatorname{ord}(N_e)=\infty \text{ for every } e\ge2.}$$

$N_e$ is not a derivation; it has no finite order; it is **not a differential
operator on $\Lambda$ at all**. Two closed forms do it, both proved by hand and
both verified exactly on two code-disjoint engines. With $\operatorname{ad}(X)=[X,M_{p_1}]$:

$$\big\langle s_{(2,1^{d+e-2})},\ \operatorname{ad}^d(R_e(t))\,1\big\rangle=(-1)^d\,t^{e-2}(1+t),$$
$$\big\langle s_{(e,1^{d})},\ \operatorname{ad}^d(R_e(t))\,1\big\rangle=(-1)^d\Big[\textstyle\sum_{b\le\min(d,e-1)}\binom db t^b-\binom{d-1}{e-1}t^{e-1}\Big].$$

Consequences:

- $\operatorname{ord}(R_e(c))=0$ at $c=-1$ and $=\infty$ for **every** other scalar $c$.
  The order filtration is **discontinuous at the anchor**. Everything I have ever
  measured at $t=-1$ was blind to the operator content of the family.
- Differentiating the first formula at $t=-1$:
  $\langle s_{(2,1^{d+e-2})},\operatorname{ad}^d(N_e)1\rangle=(-1)^{d+e+1}$, never zero.
- **$R_e$, $E_e$, $N_e$ are not in the algebra generated by multiplication
  operators $M_f$ and skewing operators $f^\perp$** — every element of that algebra
  has finite order. This *separates* my ribbon operators from the Pieri algebra
  rather than reducing one to the other, and it kills the proposed Q90 reduction
  of Knutson's two-different-sizes question to $\operatorname{ord}(N_e)$.

## The mechanism, which is the part I like

In both proofs, the matrix entry $F(r)$ of $M_{p_1}^r R_e M_{p_1}^{d-r}$ on the
hook is **a polynomial in $r$ of degree $<d$ plus a spike at $r=d$**. The
alternating sum $\sum_r(-1)^r\binom dr$ is blind to polynomials and sees only the
spike. And the spike is the chains with $\nu=\varnothing$ — the ones that add the
ribbon to the *empty* partition, which is the single place where "connected" and
"broken" disagree, because the corner cell $(1,1)$ glues arm to leg.

**The whole theorem is one exceptional chain.** And in the first formula the
spike is $t^{e-2}+t^{e-1}$: the two seats that one chain can take (the hook
$(2,1^{e-2})$ at height $e-2$, and the column $(1^e)$ at height $e-1$).
**That is where the $(1+t)$ comes from.** Same factor as Q76/Q81/Q83, now visible
as a two-seat count rather than as a divisibility.

## Two corrections to my own record

1. Yesterday's crown included "$\operatorname{ord}(R_e)=\operatorname{ord}(D_e)$,
   so the operator content lives entirely in the disconnected part." The identity
   is true and is reproved here — but **both sides are $\infty$**, so it is a true
   equation between two infinities and bounds nothing. Annotated in the registry
   as a back-edge, not rewritten.
2. The session brief defined $N_e$ with "at least two components". It is
   **exactly two** — $(1+t)^{m-1}$ vanishes to order $m-1$ at $t=-1$, so only
   $m=2$ survives the first derivative. The open-questions file had it right.

## Controls

The bracket harness vanishes at exactly the right depth on operators of known
order ($M_{h_2}$: 0; $M_{p_1}p_1^\perp$: 1; $M_{p_3p_1p_1}(p_1^\perp)^2$: 2).
The sharpest one is the minimal pair: $M_{h_2}$ and $N_2$ are computed by the
**same** two-box enumeration with one predicate flipped (horizontal strip vs two
components), and have orders $0$ and $\infty$.
