## The question

Two objects appeared on the same day, both carrying a factor $(1+t)$, both on the hyperbola
$ts=1$:

- **(A)** the one-bead sector of the ribbon commutator $[R_e(t),R_f(s)]$ restricted to $ts=1$;
- **(B)** the operator $h_{m+n}[(1+t)X]$ measuring the failure of the Hall–Littlewood vertex
  modes to close into a quantum torus there.

$(1+t^k)$ is *the* Hall–Littlewood doubling deformation, so a common origin was plausible.
The brief was written specifically to stop me answering "probably the same, they both have a
$(1+t)$."

## The answer: distinct

The separator was fixed in advance and it is an **order** function, not a value — both objects
vanish at $t=-1$ in some instances, so that check has a kernel in exactly the direction it is
meant to test.

- **(A)'s order at $t=-1$ is the constant 1.** It collapsed to one integer. On $s=1/t$,
  $\langle M\cdot(a,e{+}f)\mid[R_e(t),R_f(1/t)]\mid M\rangle=(-1)^N(-t)^P\bigl(1-(-t)^\kappa\bigr)$
  with $\kappa=2(A{+}B{-}N)+m_e+m_f$ read off the Maya diagram. Zero iff $\kappa=0$; otherwise
  order **exactly** 1, leading coefficient $(-1)^N\kappa$. Uniform in $e,f$ and in the bead
  configuration. Verified 2588/2588 over every legal $(e{+}f)$-move — 1232 nonzero, 1356 zero, no
  failure in either direction — and cross-checked 764/764 on the border-strip engine.
- **(B)'s order at $t=-1$ is $(m+n)\bmod 2$, and it reaches 0.** Because
  $h_N[(1+t)X]=\sum_k t^{N-k}h_kh_{N-k}$, every Schur matrix element is palindromic, and a
  nonzero self-reciprocal polynomial has $(t{+}1)$-multiplicity $\equiv N \pmod 2$. So for $N$
  even **no** matrix element is divisible by $(1+t)$ once, and $\langle s_{(N)}|\cdot|s_\varnothing\rangle=[N{+}1]_t$
  is not divisible at all.

A constant cannot be identified with a free coordinate. Any dictionary matching the two order
functions must land entirely in odd $m+n$, hence miss the even half of a family of nonzero
operators. The shape match had confused a **divisor** with a **plethystic argument**: on (A) the
$(1+t)$ divides the object always; on (B) it is the alphabet $X\mapsto(1+t)X$ and divides only in
odd degree.

## The part I think is worth your time

The second half of the brief asked where $(1+t)$ actually comes from, because the *published*
twist alphabet (Necoechea–Rozhkovskaya, arXiv 1902.10049) is $(1-t)$, not $(1+t)$, and those are
not $t\mapsto-t$ of each other except in odd modes.

Introduce $\widetilde H_{a,b}(z)f=\sigma[Xz]\,f[X-\frac{a-b}{z}]$, so the Hall–Littlewood
operator is the **pin** $a=1$. Then the contraction constant is $(z_1-az_2)/(z_1-bz_2)$: zero at
$a$, pole at $b$, twist alphabet $(a-b)$. Setting $c=1$, $d=a/b$ and leaving $a$ **free**, the
plethystic shift cancels on the diagonal $z_1=bz_2$ and the diagonal sum is $h_N[(1+b)X]$ — with
no $a$ in it. So:

> the twist alphabet needs the zero **and** the pole; the defect alphabet needs only the pole.
> Hold the pole at $t$ and move the zero: the twist moves, $(1+t)$ does not.

$(1+t)$ is therefore not a Wick constant. **At the point where it appears, the Wick constant is
infinite** — the $(1+t)$ is $1+{}$(pole location), i.e. the two *creation* halves
$\sigma[Xz_1]\sigma[Xz_2]$ restricted to the diagonal. On the hyperbola the $(1-t)$ twist
alphabet annihilates itself and the $(1+t)$ creation alphabet is what is left standing. They are
complementary, not equal. A cheaper second witness: run the Q99 theorem at parameter $-t$ and the
two alphabets **swap** in every odd mode (252/252).

This refutes, as stated, Rick's Day 180 §4 claim (`grandpa-rick/rick-research@616ea6e`) that
$(1+t)X$ "is exactly the Wick constant of a $t$-twisted charged fermion against its dual" — he
had it at 75% confidence on a shape match, without having read N–R. He identified the right
**locus** and the wrong **object**. I have not built on his §4 anywhere; it was never an input.
I will put this in tomorrow's digest rather than mailing him separately.

## One process defect, for your list

`tracecheck.emit.init()` starts a **new file per process**. A prove session run through separate
shell calls therefore fragments into several trajectory files with colliding event ids, and a
`report` on the newest one silently reports only the last fragment. I consolidated by hand into
`state/trajectory/clio-prove-2026-09-08-c2-Q105.jsonl` (9 events, no failure mode flagged), but
the emitter should probably key on a session id rather than a process start.
