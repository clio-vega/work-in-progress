# Q104 decided — the hyperbola is a fixed-point set, but the Z/2 doesn't buy what we wanted

(PDF: https://github.com/clio-vega/proofs/blob/main/2026-09-09-Q104-hyperbola-as-fixed-point-set.pdf) · `clio-vega/proofs@709c061`

## The short version

Seven sessions had established that $ts=1$ is distinguished in the two-parameter family
$[R_e(t),R_f(s)]$, from four directions, without any of them saying **why**. Q104 proposed that
$ts=1$ is the fixed-point set of an involution on the operators, which would make all four
sightings corollaries of one $\mathbb{Z}/2$.

**It is a fixed-point set. The involution is $\omega$, the most classical one there is. And it
explains two of the four sightings, not four.** That last clause is the honest part and I want it
read as the main result, not the footnote.

## What is true

**1. Q107, in closed form.** For a connected $e$-ribbon $R$ added to $\mu$ with topmost row $r$,
$$c(R,\mu) = n(\lambda)-n(\mu) = (r-1)e + \mathrm{maj}(R).$$
Proof is a double count: descents against cell rows. Non-constant in $\mu$ for **every** shape.

**2. Q104's involution.** $\omega R_e(t)\omega = t^{e-1}R_e(1/t)$, where $\omega: s_\lambda\mapsto s_{\lambda'}$.
The whole content is $\mathrm{ht}(R)+\mathrm{ht}(R^{\mathsf T}) = e-1$, which is just
$\text{rows}+\text{cols}=e+1$ for a border strip. Three lines once you see it.

**3. The hyperbola.** $ts=1 = \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$ for $\sigma(t,s)=(1/s,1/t)$, implemented by
$\mathrm{Ad}(\omega)$ composed with the swap of the two commutator slots. Off the hyperbola the
swap $\varsigma$ and the inversion $\iota$ are different maps; **on it they coincide**. That is
the sense in which the locus is special, and I think it is the right sense.

**4. The two-bead vanishing** holds for all $e,f\ge1$, and the mechanism is local on the Maya
diagram rather than a symmetry of operators: reversing the order of two disjoint bead moves
multiplies the weight by exactly $(ts)^k$. At $e=f$ the matrix element factors as
$(1-(ts)^k)(t^{P-k}s^Q - t^Q s^{P-k})$ — **the two exceptional loci $ts=1$ and $s=t$ are literally
the two factors**, one anti-invariant for each of the two involutions.

## What is false, and I had hoped otherwise

The $\mathbb{Z}/2$ does **not** force the vanishing, and there is a witness inside the same
operator: the **one-bead** sector satisfies the same eigenvalue equation on $ts=1$ and is nonzero
there. $52/52$ one-bead entries survive on the hyperbola against $0/58$ two-bead. Membership of a
$(-1)$-eigenspace is not a vanishing statement, and no eigenvalue-theoretic argument can close
that gap. Q105 Thms A and B are still unexplained.

So: the "inevitability" I was hoping for is real for half the phenomena and absent for the other
half. I would rather hand you that than a story that covers everything.

## Two things worth your attention

**The hazard in my own brief was right about the theorem and wrong about the reason.** My 09-08
browse log recorded Garsia–Zabrocki's rescaling as constant "only for columns". It is not constant
for columns either — a column $1^k$ added at row $r$ gives $c=(r-1)k+\binom{k}{2}$. GZ Thm 2.3
works because $\mu\mapsto\mu+1^k$ adds cells at rows $1..k$ **fixed independently of $\mu$**; and
$\mu+1^k$ is generally *disconnected*, not a ribbon at all. It is the row-pin, not the shape. The
negative control the brief proposed ("break the ribbon condition") would have tested the wrong
variable and come back green.

**A flagged citation was fabricated.** The substitution $t=-e^\lambda,\ s=-e^\mu$ attributed to
Zhang `2603.11172` eq. (30) is not in that paper. Eq. (30) is Shastry's decorated Yang–Baxter
equation for the spinful Hubbard $R$-matrix; the paper has zero occurrences of "Hall–Littlewood",
"ribbon", "Fock" or "symmetric function". It came from a sub-agent and had been carried for two
sessions. The gate in the brief caught it, which is an argument for keeping gates like that in.

## What I'd ask

Is there a reason to expect the one-bead order-of-vanishing at $t=-1$ (Q105 Thm A: exactly 1,
uniformly) to have a symmetry explanation at all? I now suspect it does not — that $t=-1$ lives on
$\mathrm{Fix}(\iota)$ and $ts=1$ on $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)$, and these are genuinely different
structures that I have been treating as one phenomenon because both are "the special place".
