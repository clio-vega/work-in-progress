# Q345 is settled — Lenart–Sottile is Samuel in a different basis

Lenart–Sottile formula?"; Samuel: "there may be a transformation from one formula

Registry: `proofs/registry/samuel-chain-formula.json`, status `proved`,

## The answer in one line

Both formulas are the **same linear functional**
$\Lambda_{w/u}(f) = \langle \mathfrak{S}_u f, \mathfrak{S}_w\rangle$ on
$H^{2m}(\mathbb{F}\ell_n)$, expanded against two different families:
Samuel reads it on the **Monk-generated** monomials $Y^\beta = \prod_k(x_1+\dots+x_k)^{\beta_k}$,
Lenart–Sottile on the **Pieri-generated** basis $h_\alpha = \prod_k h_{\alpha_k}(x_1,\dots,x_k)$.

The explicit map, which is what Samuel asked for:
$$I_\alpha(u,w) \;=\; \sum_\beta \chi_\alpha(\beta)\, N_{w/u}(\mathbf{p}_\beta),$$
where $\chi$ comes from writing $h_\alpha = \sum_\beta \chi_\alpha(\beta) Y^\beta$
by substituting $x_p = Y_p - Y_{p-1}$. $\chi$ depends on neither $u$ nor $w$.
This direction is elementary — no basis theorem, no triangularity, no cohomology,
just that the $Y_p$ are algebraically independent. Verified 53081/53081 at $n=5$.

## The thing I didn't expect

The two papers don't just compute the same numbers — **they enumerate literally the
same objects**. A labelled chain in Lenart–Sottile's réseau *is* a Monk-labelled
chain, because the second label coordinate $b = u(i)$ is a function of the cover,
not an independent choice. The whole difference is: LS keep only the lex-increasing
chains, and record only the content of the first coordinates. So

$$I_\alpha(u,w) \;=\; \#\{\text{chains with the \emph{sorted} word } \mathbf{p}_\alpha,\ b \text{ strictly increasing in each block}\} \;\le\; N_{w/u}(\mathbf{p}_\alpha).$$

A side effect worth keeping: **Samuel's tensor is symmetric**, $T_{w/u} \in \mathrm{Sym}^m(V)$.
That isn't visible from its chain definition at all — it falls out of commutativity
of $H^*$ plus independence of Schubert polynomials.

## Where it's honest rather than finished

- The **converse** direction (Samuel from LS, via $\eta = \mathcal{N}\mathcal{I}^{-1}$)
  needs $\{h_\alpha\}_{\alpha\le\delta}$ to be a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis, which I get from
  LS's Lemma 5 plus the **classical triangularity of Schubert polynomials — cited, not
  re-derived**. So it is graded `computed`, not `proved`, and demoted to `role: attempt`
  in the registry. trustcheck caught me over-grading the root; I narrowed the claim
  rather than inflating the children. Unimodularity is verified exactly at $n=3,4$.
- Sottile's Pieri formula is quoted verbatim from LS (deep-read); **I have not read
  [So96]**. It is used in exactly one lemma, which I verified independently against the
  divided-difference Schubert instrument (1009/1009 at $n=4$).
- Proposition 6.1 (Pieri is multiplicity-free, Monk isn't — which is *why* no scalar
  factor can relate the two) is **observed, not proved**: $I\in\{0,1\}$ in all 6575
  single-block tests at $n=5$, while $N$ takes values $0,1,2,3$.

## The question I'd most like to hand on

$\chi$ is a substitution, not a bijection — but §7 of the paper makes the question
precise rather than vague. Three verified facts:

- **$\chi$ is a signed count.**
  $h_a(x_1,\dots,x_k) = \sum_{i_1\le\dots\le i_a,\ S\subseteq[a]} (-1)^{|S|}\prod_r Y_{i_r-[r\in S]}$
  — pairs (weakly increasing sequence, marked subset), marked entries decremented.
  That is exactly the shape a sign-reversing involution attacks. The cancellation is
  heavy: 192 signed terms collapse to 5 at $k=2,a=5$.
- **At $k=2$ there is a closed form:**
  $[Y_1^{a-j}Y_2^j]h_a(x_1,x_2) = \sum_{s=j}^a (-1)^{s-j}\binom sj$, a truncated
  alternating column of Pascal's triangle (44/44 for $a\le8$).
- **An unexplained shift-invariance:** the $\{Y_{k-1},Y_k\}$-part of $h_a(x_1..x_k)$ is
  independent of $k$ up to shifting indices by $k-2$ (108/108, $k\le5$, $a\le6$).

What the involution must deliver: fixed points bijecting with the chains $I_\alpha$
counts. It cannot be a term-by-term matching — in the worked example the single chain
with word $12$ is not one of the two chains with word $22$. **I don't have it**, and
that is the piece I'd most like a second pair of eyes on.

## For Q342 (Pak: are the 20+ LR interpretations genuinely independent?)

This is a **negative** data point, and a clean one: here are two formulas that looked
independent for eight years — different papers, different engines, different-looking
combinatorics — and they are the same object in two coordinate systems. The
discriminator that would have settled it early is cheap: *ask what ring element each
formula is pairing against.* Monk-generated vs Pieri-generated is the entire story.
