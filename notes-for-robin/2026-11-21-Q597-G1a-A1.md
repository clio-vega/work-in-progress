## What α is
α is the displacement of the clean journey's first middle step. It is the clean target's own frame vector:
- α = u_A whenever the clean journey goes to C (253/253);
- α = w_A whenever it goes to B (268/268);
- the size-6 subsample agrees (506/506).

These are the lattice vectors of the engine's directions d_C and d_B. So the value is a one-journey quantity. What is genuinely two-journey is *why* the first middle step has that displacement.

## Proved
- **Thm red (by hand).** (A1) holds iff that step is the "straight" step (class 1, a1 for B / a0 for C). The proof is read off the displacement table of the 12 good configurations.
- **Lemma hw (by hand).** In the start class, a journey's a-steps shift its 0-triangles by −w_A and its 1-triangles by −u_A, for either target. So α compensates the shift of the half-wake that the clean journey crosses.
- **Lemma nr (computer-assisted, every size).** A journey never re-creates a tile it removed. Monotone 4-offsets give a window of 9 steps, and all words of length ≤ 10 give 0 cases in 3.0M pairs. This is the converse to last week's no-revisit theorem.
- **Thm main.** (A1) ⇐ (FC≤) ∧ (P0) ∧ (P1). Its pieces:
  - divergence = first contact with the other journey's net-removed set W;
  - no contact in the prefix (proved);
  - no shared rhombus position before contact (proved);
  - two exhaustive local tables (square-sharing and triangle-sharing).

## What is left (all computed 521/521, and 506/506 on the size-6 subsample)
- (FC≤): the divergence step touches W.
- (P0): the contact is one square plus one triangle.

A two-word model shows that tiling consistency alone does not force the conclusion: 308/1230 contacts are not straight. Exactly those violate (P1). So (P1) and last session's (A2) look like a single parity question: what class is the other journey in where the clean journey reads its wake?
