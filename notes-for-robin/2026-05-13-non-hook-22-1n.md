## Headline

Closes one more infinite family of the rank-zero theorem ---
**the first non-hook one**.  Same template as the May-11 and May-12
proofs, with the new wrinkle that we now have to handle 2-block
$+q$-eigenvectors in *both* directions of the inductive basis (column
2 and column 1 corners), not just column 1 as in the hook case.

## The result

For $\lambda = (2, 2, 1^{n-4})$ and every $n \ge 6$:

> $R'_{n-1} R'_n V_\lambda$ is one-dimensional, supported on the four
> SYTs indexed by $\{(n-3, n-2), (n-3, n-1), (n-2, n), (n-1, n)\}$,
> all in $E_1^-$.

Hence $\Pi^{S_n}|_{V_\lambda} = 0$ unconditionally for every $n \ge 6$.

Verified computationally at $n = 6, 7, 8, 9$ via
`~/projects/scratch/2026-05-13-non-hook-22-1n/verify_proof.py`.

## Mechanism

**Phase A** ($R'_n$): The image $W_j^{(n)}$ at each step is exactly
$E_j^+$, with explicit basis $\mathcal{B}_j = \{w_j(a) : a \in
\{2,\ldots,j-1\}\} \cup \{w_j(c) : c \in \{j+2,\ldots,n\}\}$.  Each
basis vector is a $T_j$ $2$-block $+q$-eigenvector with support of
size $2$ (or degenerate single).  At each step, $(T_j + 1)$ acts as a
clean relabeling: $w_{j-1}(a) \mapsto w_j(a)$ for $a \le j-2$;
$w_{j-1}(c=j+1) \mapsto w_j(a=j-1)$ ("the slot is shifted into the
$a$-side"); $w_{j-1}(c) \mapsto w_j(c)$ for $c \ge j+2$.

The dimension count $\dim E_j^+ = n-3$ is pinned down via
trace conjugacy: all $T_j$ have the same trace on $V_\lambda$,
combined with eigenvalue $\{q, -1\}$ and direct count at $j = 1$.

After $R'_n$: $W_n = E_{n-1}^+$, basis $\{w_{n-1}(a) : a = 2,\ldots,
n-2\}$ where $w_{n-1}(a) = \alpha v_{(a,n-1)} + \beta v_{(a,n)}$.

**Phase B** ($R'_{n-1}$):
- *Step 1*: $(T_1 + 1)$ kills $w_{n-1}(a)$ for $a \ge 3$ (since these
have $a \ge 3$, hence $2$ in column 1, hence $\in E_1^-$).  Survives:
$w_{n-1}(2)$, 1-dim.
- *Steps 2..n-3*: shifting window.  Image is 1-dim with support
$\{(j, n-1), (j+1, n-1), (j, n), (j+1, n)\}$, all four coefficients
nonzero.  Inductive argument: the previous support contains
$v_{(j-1, *)}$ killed by $(T_j+1)$ (column 1 same), and $v_{(j, *)}$
in a $T_j$ 2-block coupling with $v_{(j+1, *)}$.
- *Step n-2*: terminal.  Three cases get killed (column 2 same or
column 1 same), and two of the four supports survive in different
2-blocks of $T_{n-2}$.  Final support is 4-element:
$\{(n-3, n-2), (n-3, n-1), (n-2, n), (n-1, n)\}$.  All have min $\ge
n-3 \ge 3$, hence in $E_1^-$.

## Comparison with hook $(3, 1^{n-3})$

| Family | $\ell + 1$ | Final support size | Catalan? |
|---|---|---|---|
| $(2, 1^{n-2})$ | $n$ | 2 | $C_2$ |
| $(3, 1^{n-3})$ | $n-1$ | 5 | $C_3$ |
| $(2, 2, 1^{n-4})$ | $n-1$ | **4** | not $C_k$ |

The non-hook has one *fewer* SYT in the support: namely $(n-2, n-1)$,
which has $a = n-2, c = n-1$ in column 2 (same column 2 → killed by
$(T_{n-2} + 1)$).  In the hook case, the analogous SYT
$T = T_{(n-2, n-1)}$ has $\{n-2, n-1\}$ both in row 1, so $(T_{n-2}+1)$
acts as $(1+q)$ on it (same-row +q eigenvector) → survives.

## Catalan-products conjecture

Inspired by this, I'm conjecturing (Conj 8.1 in writeup):

> For $\lambda \vdash n$ in the rank-zero regime, the support size of
> the spanning vector of $B_{\ell+1}V_\lambda$ is a product of
> Catalan-like numbers $\binom{2k_i - 2}{k_i - 1}$ over the
> length-preserving corners of $\lambda$, suitably modified.

For $\lambda = (2, 2, 1^{n-4})$: one corner of "size $k = 2$" (giving
the column-2 contribution).  Conjecturally $\binom{2}{1} \cdot C_2 = 2
\cdot 2 = 4$.  Matches!

I'm not confident the formula is right, but the structural pattern
(non-hook = hook $\times$ "boundary correction" per corner) is
suggestive.  Worth verifying on $\lambda = (2, 2, 2, 1^{n-6})$ next
session.

## What's open

discussed the obstruction: the second factor of $R'_{n-1}$ leaves the
image at dim $\binom{n-3}{1} = n-3$, not 1, requiring a multidimensional
shifting argument.
2. Structural proof for $\lambda = (2, 2, 2, 1^{n-6})$ (next non-hook
test case).  Would let us test Conj 8.1.
3. Combinatorial characterization of the Catalan support family for
$k = 3, 4$ hooks (May-12 open question 1, still open).
4. The relationship between Phase A and the $V_\lambda \downarrow_{S_{n-1}}$
branching --- there might be a cleaner branching-based proof avoiding
the case enumeration.

## Honesty check

The Phase A induction is a direct case-by-case computation, and I
had to think carefully about the boundary cases ($j = 2$ and $j = 3$
where $\mathcal{B}_j$ has degenerate behavior at $a = 2$).  The
argument is solid but not aesthetically clean ---
specifically, my proof of $\mathcal{B}_j$ basis transformation
proceeds by checking each of three sub-cases (I, II, III in
Lemma 4.4 of the writeup).  A more unified treatment would be nicer.

The trace conjugacy argument for $\dim E_j^+ = n-3$ is much cleaner
than my original approach (case-by-case enumeration of same-row,
same-column counts).

The Catalan-products conjecture is genuinely speculative --- 1 data
point ($(2, 2, 1^{n-4})$) for non-hooks isn't enough.  The next
prove session should test it on $(2, 2, 2, 1^{n-6})$.
