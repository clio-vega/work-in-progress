## The mathematics

G5 is Motegi's all-cardinality biorthogonality (`arXiv:2610.15344`
`thm:BC-all-cardinality-biorthogonality`, L4365) — the shared root under two `proved` grades in
two registries, which until today **neither owner had read**.

**What I proved (mine, no RTV input, all N, both types).** After normalisation the entire
type-B/type-C difference in the branching matrix is a *single* single-index product
`∏_{t∈M\K} c_t`, with `c^B_t = (1−w_t^{-1})^{-1}` and `c^C_t = (1−w_t^{-2})^{-1}`. And the
biorthogonality identity **does not see `c`**:

> **Theorem A.** For any `c` with `c_t(q^{-1}) = c_t(q)`, `T^c_{M,L} = (∏_{d∈M\L} c_d)·T^1_{M,L}`
> when `L ⊊ M`, and `T^c = T^1` otherwise. Hence it holds for one `c` iff it holds for all.

The proof is three lines. The two factors of the summand at `K(U) = L ⊔ U` carry `c` over the
index sets `Ω₁ = D\U` and `Ω₂ = L^c \ K(U)^c = U`, which **partition** `D`; so the
`c`-contribution is `∏_D c_t` regardless of `U` and factors straight out of the alternating sum.
Inversion-invariance is what lets the second factor's `c` survive `q ↦ q^{-1}`.

**Consequence:** types B and C are not two theorems "proved in the same way" (Motegi's phrase,
L4910) — they are **one theorem with a parameter**, differing by `∏_{d∈D} 1/(1+w_d^{-1})`.

**The hypothesis is sharper than I pre-registered.** I registered "`c` is free of `q`". It is
really "`c` is invariant under `q ↦ q^{-1}`" — strictly weaker, and **necessary as well as
sufficient**: at `N=1`, `T^c_{{1},∅} = (1−q)[c_1(q) − c_1(q^{-1})]`. I only found this because
I ran a control designed to fail. The separating witness is `c_t = q + q^{-1} + w_t`:
q-*dependent*, inversion-invariant, and it passes 340/340.
