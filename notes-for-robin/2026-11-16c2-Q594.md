# Q594 (PROVE 2026-11-16 c2): mixed commutation is not transported tableau switching

Robin, this note closes the question I half-asked you on 11-15 ("is there a gashes-commute lemma?"). **Short answer: no lemma in the literature covers it.** I read the three candidates first-hand in their TeX:

- **Buch 1401.3065, Thm `thm:mutate`.** The two gashes produced by resolving one temporary piece have *disjoint* propagation paths, and Φ²=id. That is disjointness, and in my data disjoint pairs are exactly the trivial ones.
- **BKPT 1401.1725, Lemma `lem:chainorder`.** It swaps *nested* steps of one Pieri chain. That is a same-direction statement, and it is vacuous at one step (Cor `cor:label012`).
- **ACM 2501.01947, App. A.** The migration ↔ switching dictionary is whole-flock and given by example. It is not stepwise.

**What the data says.** Take the window of a migration to be the set of triangles it pushes. Then:
- Two same-direction (B/B) migrations commute **iff their windows are disjoint**: 339/339. All 440 failures overlap, never nested.
- Mixed (B/C) migrations commute even when their windows overlap: 779/779. That overlap case is the whole content of MC, and no published lemma touches it.

**A byproduct you might know the answer to.** In the Cayley-trick picture, a mosaic is a fine mixed subdivision of aΔ₀+bΔ₁. Computationally, it is determined by the positions of its 1-triangles alone (1979/1979 mosaics over 9 hexagons, with a control that fails 141 times). I can prove the ribbon structure, but not the uniqueness: the gap is vertices of P₀ whose fibre has no area. *Is "a fine mixed subdivision of a sum of two triangles is determined by its (1,2)-cells" a known theorem?* I could not browse this session.

Status: MC is not proved, and G1 is still conditional on MC+SW.
