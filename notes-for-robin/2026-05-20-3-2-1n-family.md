# For Robin — 2026-05-20: rank-zero theorem for $(3,2,1^{n-5})$, first **two-corner** family

## TL;DR

$\Pi^{S_n}|_{V_{(3,2,1^{n-5})}} = 0$ for every $n \ge 8$. Structurally proved.

Writeup: `2026-05-20-3-2-1n-family.tex` (7pp).

This was the backup PROVE.md target flagged in the May-19 for-robin
note. The empirical surprise from earlier sessions was that
$B_{\ell+1}^{(\lambda)} V_\lambda$ has dim **$2$, not $1$**, so the
$j$-formula at $j = \ell + 1$ doesn't collapse to a $1$-dim line.
The resolution: even though dim $= 2$, the **whole image still lies
in $E_1^-$**, which is all the rank-zero theorem requires. The
$(T_1{+}1)$ at the start of $R'_\ell$ annihilates it.

## Why this works — the three-branch picture

$\lambda = (3, 2, 1^{n-5})$ has three removable corners:
- $c_1 = (1, 3)$ — length-preserving (row 1)
- $c_2 = (2, 2)$ — length-preserving (row 2)
- $c_3 = (n-3, 1)$ — column-$1$ bottom (length-reducing)

The $T_{n-1}$-2-blocks in $V_\lambda$ are indexed by **pairs** of
corners. There are three pairs, giving three branching isos:

| Branch | Pair                | Doubly-stripped $\mu_X$ ($\vdash n-2$) | $j(\mu_X)$ | $B_{n-3}^{(\mu_X)} V_{\mu_X}$ |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------------------------|------------|-------------------------------|
| $A$    | $\{c_1, c_3\}$      | $(2, 2, 1^{n-6})$                      | $n-3$      | dim $1$, $\subseteq E_1^-$    |
| $B$    | $\{c_2, c_3\}$      | $(3, 1^{n-5})$                         | $n-3$      | dim $1$, $\subseteq E_1^-$    |
| $C$    | $\{c_1, c_2\}$      | $(2, 1^{n-4})$                         | $n-2$      | $= 0$                         |

Each $\mu_X$ has a known $j$-formula:
- $\mu_A$ is in the May-15/18 $(2^a, 1^{n-2a})$ family at $a = 2$.
- $\mu_B$ is the May-12 column-1 hook with first part $3$.
- $\mu_C$ is the May-11 column-1 hook with first part $2$.

The May-15 commutation reduction extends *verbatim* to multiple
branches:
$$
B_{n-2}^{(\lambda)} V_\lambda
= (T_{n-3}{+}1)(T_{n-2}{+}1) \sum_X \Phi_X\big(B_{n-3}^{(\mu_X)} V_{\mu_X}\big).
$$

The crucial new feature: the third branch $\mu_C$ has its $j$-formula
*one index higher* ($j(\mu_C) = n - 2 \ne n - 3$). So
$B_{n-3}^{(\mu_C)} V_{\mu_C} = R'^{(\mu_C)}_{n-3} \cdot B_{n-2}^{(\mu_C)}
V_{\mu_C}$ — the rightmost $(T_1{+}1)$ of $R'^{(\mu_C)}_{n-3}$ kills
the $E_1^-$ subspace from $B_{n-2}^{(\mu_C)}$. The $C$-piece vanishes.

The remaining two pieces ($A$, $B$) each contribute a 1-dim image in
$E_1^-$, giving total dim $\le 2$ in $E_1^-$. Hence $\Pi^{S_n} V_\lambda
= 0$.

## Why this is a structural milestone

This is the **first** rank-zero proof in the program where
$B_{\ell+1}^{(\lambda)}$ is **not** $1$-dimensional. All prior
proofs (hooks, $(2^a, 1^{n-2a})$, etc.) had a single
length-preserving corner and dim $= 1$. The rank-corner formula
predicts dim $=$ number of length-preserving corners (still
conjectural in general).

The three-branch framework here is the natural generalization. For
general $\lambda$ with $r$ length-preserving corners $c_1, \ldots,
c_r$, the picture should be:
- $\binom{r+1}{2}$ branches total (pairing length-pres × length-pres
  and length-pres × column-1 bottom).
- The $r$ branches involving the bottom contribute non-zero
  (each via the rank-zero theorem for the doubly-stripped
  $\mu$).
- The $\binom{r}{2}$ length-pres × length-pres branches contribute
  $0$ (because their doubly-stripped $\mu$ has $j$-formula one
  step later than needed, leaking via the $E_1^-$ wall).

This **predicts the rank-corner formula** structurally:
$\dim B_{\ell+1}^{(\lambda)} V_\lambda = r$.

the natural conjecture.

## Honest assessment

**Rigorously proved:**
- Three-branch reduction formula (Theorem 2 in writeup).
- $E_1^-$ containment of $B_{n-2}^{(\lambda)} V_\lambda$, hence
  rank-zero (Theorem 4).

**Not proved (a finite case analysis would close it):**
- $\dim B_{n-2}^{(\lambda)} V_\lambda = 2$ exactly (the lower bound).
  We have $\le 2$ from the reduction; non-vanishing of each of the
  $A$- and $B$-piece images after $(T_{n-3}{+}1)(T_{n-2}{+}1)$
  would close this. This is finite Hoefsmit case analysis
  analogous to May-15 Section 5. The dim claim isn't needed for
  rank-zero, so I left it for a future writeup.
