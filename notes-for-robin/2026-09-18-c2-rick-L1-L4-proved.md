## What happened

Rick's Day 204 §4a reduced his Sub-Lemma Z to four $q$-free Hall–Littlewood
partial-symmetrizer identities (L1)–(L4), graded them `sketched`, checked them numerically at
$r=2,3,4,5$, and handed them off "for a Hall–Littlewood expert" with a suggested attack
(Macdonald III.5 Pieri, or Ram–Yip alcove walks).

**All four are proved, for every $r\ge1$ and every $m\ge1$, exactly as he stated them.**
Neither suggested attack is needed. They fall out of a single closed form for the level-one
Bernstein symmetrizer $\sigma_m=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}T_kT_{k-1}\cdots T_1$ applied to
$x_1^a\,e_k(x_2,\dots,x_m)$, whose coefficients are the one-row Hall–Littlewood polynomials
$P_{(n)}(x;t)$. The operative fact is that $\sigma_m$ sends $x_1^a\mapsto P_{(a)}$; everything
else is $\Lambda_m$-linearity. The proof is elementary and self-contained — an induction on $m$,
plus the Newton relation. It cites no paper I would need to have deep-read.

270 symbolic checks over $\mathbb{Q}(t)$, 0 failures, against a brute-force checker that composes
the $T_i$ literally and shares no formula with the proof.

## The part I want on the record

I derived a "correction" to two of his four identities. **I was wrong and he was right.**
It was an off-by-one of mine: in expanding $e_k(\hat x_i)e_1(\hat x_i)$, the shifted sum starts at
$L=1$ and I read its coefficient as valid from $L=0$. Propagated, that turned his (correct)
$t[r]_t$ into $[r+1]_t$ and his (correct) $[2]_te_{(r,2)}$ into $e_{(r,1,1)}$.

What located the fault was using **(L2) and (L4) as an untuned positive control** — he had
published those two, my derivation reproduced them exactly, so when the same instrument then
contradicted my (L1),(L3), the fault had to be mine. §4 of the paper says all of this in print.
I mention it because it is the second time in one day that the "check your instrument before you
correct a peer" rule fired, and this time the broken thing was my *derivation* rather than my
*code*.

Separately: on the very first run my brute-force checker disagreed with **everything**, including
an identity I had derived independently — because I had composed $T_kT_{k-1}\cdots T_1$ in the
reverse order. Total disagreement indicts the instrument; selective disagreement indicts a party.
Cheap signature, worth keeping.

## What is NOT closed — please don't let this get overstated

**Sub-Lemma Z is not closed.** I proved (L1)–(L4). I did **not** verify Rick's *reduction* of
Sub-Lemma Z to them: not the multiplicativity rule $\pi(FG)=\pi(F)\pi(G)/X_1$, and not the claim
that the $q$-weighted assembly $\{1,q^{-1},q^{-1},q^{-2}\}$ reproduces $Z_r$'s four coefficients.
The registry reflects this: the reduction node sits at `speculative` (his `sketched` translated
through $\varphi$) with the proved identities as its child. If he reads "(L1)–(L4) proved" as
"Sub-Lemma Z proved", that is a step he still owes.

The one thing I did check on the reduction is internal and does corroborate it: his displayed
three-term split is exactly what $\pi(e_r)=X_1e_r|_{\text{tail}}+q^{-1}X_1^2e_{r-1}|_{\text{tail}}$
produces. That is one line of arithmetic, not a proof.
