## The theorem (PROVE, this morning)

$\operatorname{ord}(N_e)=\infty$. Sharper: $R_e(c)$ (for $c\ne-1$), $E_e(c)$ and $N_e$ lie in **no
algebra generated by** multiplication operators $M_f$ and skewing operators $f^{\perp}$, since every
element of that algebra has finite order. Registry `Q84-no-pieri-presentation` (`proved`),
`proofs/2026-09-06-Q84-order-of-N-e.tex`, `proofs@7a111aa`.

And the sting: $\operatorname{ord}R_e(c)=0$ at $c=-1$ and $=\infty$ at every other scalar. The point
where the whole programme is anchored — $R_e(-1)=M_{p_e}$, Murnaghan–Nakayama, every dictionary
check I have run — is the one point where the operator content vanishes identically.

## What last night's reading adds

The literature has exactly one normal form for an operator on $\Lambda$:
$\sum_\lambda(\text{mult by }g_\lambda)\circ f_\lambda^{\perp}$. Nazarov–Sklyanin
(`arXiv:1212.2781` eq. 23, `1212.2960` eq. 26), Graf (`2511.01114`), Wang (`2602.04779`); Hardt's
survey (`2109.14597` line 407) writes down $M_{h_k}$ and $h_k^\perp$ side by side and **never
discusses order at all**. Every *finite* such expression has finite order. So the theorem reads:
**nobody wrote this operator down because it is not expressible in the form everyone writes
operators in.** That is a novelty warrant with a mechanism, rather than another keyword null.

But the exclusion covers only the finite regime, and that is the interesting part. NS's own
$A^{(1)}$ is written *both* as their $\ell(\lambda)=1$ sum *and* as a **product of two
exponentials** (`1212.2960`, source line 485) — unbounded order. Bernstein's $S_m$, Jing's
Hall–Littlewood vertex operator, and Zabrocki's cell-by-cell ribbon recursion
$S^{R_+}=\widehat{\widehat{S^R}S_1}$ (`math/0008188`) all live there. So my reading is:

## Why I think you'll care

**The lattice-model question changes sign.** I had been reading "not a differential operator" as an
obstacle to finding a vertex model for $g_e$. It is the opposite. Lattice models produce symmetric
functions *through* vertex operators, so an operator of infinite order is exactly what a vertex
model would predict. Q86 (does the spin Hall–Littlewood model see the component count $m$?) is now
a prediction to test rather than a hope — and it sits on the Wheeler–Zinn-Justin shelf that your
cylindric work already borders.

Concretely, the attack I like best: look for a **flux** in Knutson–Zinn-Justin's sense — an additive
quantity on the gaps between beads of the Maya diagram, incremented by a local bilinear as a ribbon
passes, with $\Phi_W+\Phi_S=\Phi_E+\Phi_N$. That conservation law is the two-hop locality I already
proved for nested ribbons. If the flux exists, order questions become flux-counting questions.
