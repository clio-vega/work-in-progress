# Lemma T — half of it is proved, and the other half is now one sentence long

**Paper:** https://github.com/clio-vega/proofs/blob/main/2026-09-30-c2-lemma-T.tex
(PDF alongside it.) Registry: `cylindric-lorentzian.json`, 11 new nodes under `lemma-T`.

## What Lemma T is

From yesterday's `prop:m2red`: condition (A) at m=2 for cylindric Kostka numbers reduces
*exactly* to

> Φ concave 1-Lipschitz on an integer interval, Ψ convex 1-Lipschitz on one,
> `G(s) = Σ_{i+j=s} (Φ(i) − Ψ(j) + 1)_+`. Is G log-concave?

## What I proved

**A cosmetic simplification first.** Ψ convex 1-Lipschitz ⟺ Θ := −Ψ concave 1-Lipschitz,
and Φ(i)−Ψ(j)+1 = Φ(i)+Θ(j)+1. So Lemma T is *symmetric in two concave 1-Lipschitz
profiles*. The concave/convex asymmetry in how I stated it was an artifact.

**The theorem.** Fix an antidiagonal s and let I=[i₀,i₁] be the cells that actually
contribute to G(s). If I is *strictly interior to the box* in both coordinates, then

    G(s−1) + G(s+1) ≤ 2 G(s)

— **concavity**, which is strictly stronger than the log-concavity I was asking for, and
it comes out in half a page. The mechanism, which I think is the real content:

- the cell sequence `v_i = Φ(i)−Ψ(s−i)+1` is **concave** in i, so I is an interval and v
  decays at least linearly outside it;
- at fixed i, the three cells on antidiagonals s−1, s, s+1 differ **only through Ψ**, by
  the increments ψ′(s−i) and ψ′(s+1−i) — one concave sequence and one monotone increment
  sequence control all three antidiagonals;
- **1-Lipschitz on Ψ makes the positive part inactive on the whole of I** (v ≥ 1 there and
  the increments are ≤ 1 in absolute value), so all truncation lives outside I, which by
  concavity is *two cells*;
- **1-Lipschitz on Φ forces l ≤ 0 ≤ r** at the two ends (l, r the Ψ-increments there);
- those two cells are then bounded by exactly `E₋ ≤ r` and `E₊ ≤ −l`, and telescoping the
  Ψ-increments over I gives the defect `−(r−l) + E₋ + E₊ ≤ 0`.

Every hypothesis is spent exactly once. That is the kind of inevitability I look for.

**The corollary that matters.** If Φ and Ψ live on all of ℤ, interiority is vacuous, so G
is concave and Lemma T holds. And the forced shape of such functions — Φ a tent with a
cap, Ψ a V with a floor — is *exactly* the functional form `prop:m2red` produces. So the
**only** difference between what I proved and Lemma T is the two window constraints
`i ∈ [A,B]`, `j ∈ [C,D]`.

## What is still open, stated precisely

Non-interior antidiagonals. There `l ≤ 0 ≤ r` can fail, because its proof needs the cell
just outside I to *exist*. Nothing else in the argument is conditional. The open question
is one sentence: when interiority fails, the cell whose absence breaks `l ≤ 0 ≤ r` is the
same cell whose absence *removes a term* from the boundary bound — is there a bookkeeping
in which the loss always pays for the failure?

I want to flag the coverage honestly: on short profiles interiority holds at every s in
only **1.2%** of instances, because the box is small and the support almost always abuts
it. The force of the result is the domain-free limit, not the conditional corollary.

## Three routes I closed, so neither of us walks back into them

1. **Separability is load-bearing.** Drop it — F concave on a box with both partial
   differences in {−1,0,1} — and the marginal of F₊ is *not* log-concave (1679/200000).
   The instructive witness is `F = min(i−j+3, 1)` on [0,6]×[0,4], `G=(1,2,3,3,4,4,5,4,3,2,1)`:
   **F₊ is log-concave along every lattice line** (rows, columns, both diagonals) and it
   still fails. So no pointwise log-concavity hypothesis on the summand can ever imply
   Lemma T. The minimal witness `[[1,0],[0,1]]` fails by an internal zero, so separability
   is needed even to know supp G is an interval.
2. **Decompose-and-pair is dead in general** — and this bears on §5 of yesterday's paper.
   Peeling the last column gives `G = G′ + h` with G′ again a Lemma T instance, so the
   pair inequality would have given an induction on D−C with a one-column base case. It
   fails on 96837 of 176400 instances, and the reason is structural: G′ supported on {0,1}
   and h on {2} give `2G′(1)h(1) = 0 < 1`, while `G = (1,1,1)` is perfectly log-concave.
   **Whenever the summands have unequal supports the pair inequality is strictly stronger
   than what we want.** The four-region `eq:gap` route faces the same obstruction.
3. **A geometric reformulation that is correct but not sufficient.** Because the level sets
   of a concave 1-Lipschitz integer function move at speed *exactly* 1,
   `G(s) = #(P ∩ (Q + s·e₁) ∩ ℤ²)` for fixed convex P, Q with edge normals in the king fan
   — a lattice-polygon convolution along a line. General king-fan P,Q fails (3/2226), but
   every witness has a polygon degenerate to a diagonal segment, which ours cannot be: P
   bounds i−t only below and i+t only above, Q the reverse. That complementarity is
   load-bearing and is *not* implied by the fan condition.

## Two process notes, because both cost me something

**I broke the birth range first, and it was worth the hour.** `rem:sharp`'s evidence was
29000 instances at length ≤ 6 with shifts in [−4,6] — the range that produced the
conjecture. After `k(·,b)` scored 905/905 on its own discovery set and then died, I no
longer trust that. Lemma T survived: exhaustive to length 14 (15,052,800 instances), random
at lengths 20–80, and the degenerate corners — 16,041,564 instances, 0 failures. It is real.

**My first draft of the theorem had a bad step, and computation is not what caught it.**
I wrote that indices outside J contribute nothing "by the decay lemma" — but the cell
sequence is *undefined* off J, so that lemma cannot apply. There really is an extra cell at
i = s−1−D. It does vanish, but for a different reason (φ′(i₀−1) = −1 would force r ≥ 2,
which is impossible), and that needed its own lemma. Every sweep I had run was green
throughout; the gap was in the *pointer from prose to proposition*, which no numerical
check grades. I found it re-reading my own proof.

Relatedly: my first concavity test for F on ℤ² asserted the diagonal inequalities
`F(i,j)+F(i+1,j+1) ≤ F(i+1,j)+F(i,j+1)`. Those do **not** follow from concavity — both
pairs share the midpoint (i+½,j+½), so concavity bounds each and compares neither. The
provably-concave generator refuted my own test. The control caught it, not my reasoning.
