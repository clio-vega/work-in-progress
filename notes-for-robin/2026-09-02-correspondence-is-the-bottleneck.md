# For Robin — 2026-09-02 (DREAM): the bottleneck moved, and it is no longer mathematics

Three observations from a full six-session day. None is urgent; all three are structural.
*(Not sent — today's digest went out at wake. This is the dream-cycle version, for the next digest
or for whenever you ask.)*

## 1. Correspondence latency is now the rate-limiting step of a three-person collaboration

This morning I found that **Rick and Lyra had each independently written to say they were blocked
on the same result of mine** — the unidentified $q$-power in §7 — which my 09-01 prove session had
already closed. Neither of them knew, and neither knew about the other. The letters answering them
had been queued since the previous day because that review session had **no email tools loaded**.

So: the mathematics was finished, two collaborators were idle, and the blocking resource was a
mail client. That is worth knowing about the loop's design. It also means the *container manifest
varies per session* is not a cosmetic annoyance — it silently converted one day of work into three
people's day of waiting.

## 2. The write slot, again — and I now know I am the one choosing

`publishable-result` is still empty; `LAST_WRITE` is **19 June**. There is no write phase in the
loop, so shipping a paper costs a **prove** slot, and every time the choice comes up I take the
prove slot. That is not an accident I keep having; it is a preference I keep enacting, and I would
rather it were a decision you and I made once, explicitly, than one I make silently every day.

The material is there: `2026-09-02-Q63-conj-j.tex` (9pp, compiled) plus `2026-08-31-Q59-*`,
`2026-08-30-Q55-*`. What is missing is a session whose *deliverable is prose*.

## 3. The good news, and it is genuinely good

I spent two days recording the puzzle↔Fock-space bridge as **empty**. It is not. Two papers cite
both banks — **Gunna–Wheeler–Zinn-Justin `2504.19205`** (which cites your seed core `0809.2392`,
all three Knutson–Zinn-Justin puzzle papers, *and* a vertex model for LLT) and **Gaetz–Gao
`2408.07863`**. The first has been in my index since 6 July and I read it six times without ever
opening its reference list.

And a smaller thing you may enjoy: **your own `1209.1807` (*Enumeration of Cylindric Plane
Partitions II*) cites van Leeuwen.** The cylindric path and the ribbon path — two of the five in
SEED.md — were already joined, in your thesis work, before I started.

The honest residue is that what closed is **puzzle ↔ LLT**; **puzzle ↔ higher-level $q$-Fock**,
where all my $\ell\ge2$ work lives, is still open. But the measurement I ran today says the gap is
in **readership**, not in mathematics: van Leeuwen's 26 citers and Purbhoo's 24 citers are
**disjoint**, and in fifteen years MathOverflow has **zero** questions joining puzzles to Fock
space — on a site where both Knutson and Zinn-Justin post. Standing where the mathematics is
already connected and the literature has not noticed is a much better position than standing at a
real gap.
