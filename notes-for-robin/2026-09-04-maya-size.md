**Short version.** `AbacusRibbon.lean` had a caveat in its own header: the bead set was a
`Finset ℤ`, not the genuine cofinite Maya set, so `sum_addRibbon` was "a *shadow* of
|μ| = |λ| + e, not a proof". That is now a theorem:

```lean
theorem TworowD4Kernel.Maya.size_addRibbon {e : ℕ} {M : Maya} {b : ℤ} (he : 0 < e)
    (hb : b ∈ M.carrier) (hbe : b + (e : ℤ) ∉ M.carrier) :
    (M.addRibbon e b).size = M.size + e
```

Sorry-free; `lake build` 2975 jobs; `lake test` green; axioms within the standard allowlist.

**The one thing I'd point at.** I expected to spend the session fighting `Set.Finite`
side-conditions — the brief even set a 45-minute abort against exactly that — and it never
became work, because of a single choice. A Maya diagram's condition "finite above and cofinite
below" is *literally* `(M ∆ vacuum).Finite`, one condition rather than two. And once it is
phrased that way, a bead move `b ↦ b+e` **is itself a symmetric difference**, so associativity
of `∆` gives `(moveBead e M b) ∆ vacuum ⊆ (M ∆ vacuum) ∪ {b, b+e}` with no case analysis at
all. The same reframing kills the three-way split on whether the bead crosses the vacuum line:
sum both sizes over a common window and the vacuum weights cancel identically.

**And the cost of that, which I want to be explicit about.** The proof is *blind* to the vacuum
line. That is exactly why it can't be trusted to tell me the three regimes (bead above the line,
crossing it, below it) are inhabited — so each is witnessed separately, with side conditions
checked, and there's a negative control showing that dropping `b + e ∉ M` makes the statement
false rather than merely weaker.
