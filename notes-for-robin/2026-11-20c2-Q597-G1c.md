# For Robin — Q597 G1c, PROVE 2026-11-20 c2

Robin, here is the short version.

**The brief's mechanism failed, and the failure was informative.** I expected the two-height potential ψ that proved no-revisit this morning to keep x's journey away from u−α, the untouched neighbour of an (A)-tile u. It does not: 0 of 1639 cases. The reason is structural. u−α sits *above* u in ψ, and the rhombus sweeps downward. So in the *past*, u−α is exactly where a swept tile would be. ψ is a one-way instrument.

**What does work:**
1. **Removal is ψ-antitone (by hand).** Tiles removed later have smaller ψ, componentwise. The one new piece is a "dip" lemma, checked against the three hexagon types. It gives the future half of G1c for free.
2. **A journey is a word in {a0, a1, b}.** Each (rhombus class, step type) has exactly one good configuration; all 36,843 real steps match exactly one. The 4-offset (ψ−μ, lateral coordinate) is monotone, and that bounds the distance between the two removals by 11 steps. An exhaustive enumeration of every word of length ≤ 12 then shows: **the ascending R-edge neighbour of a swept tile is never swept, at any time**. There were 0 violations in 33.5M step pairs, and the control fires 708k times.
3. **For every triangle there is a picture proof.** In one lattice the swept cells form a path that advances one cell index at a time and moves through strips in one direction only. The ascending neighbour lies one strip against that direction, so it is never on the path. Squares rest on the enumeration alone, which I ran twice: once on table configurations, once on configurations taken from the engine.

**Result.** G1c is proved, given one identification from G1a that I computed (1639/1639): y's displacement −α is an ascending edge of x's rhombus at the step where x removed u.

**What remains for WS** is therefore sharply a *two-journey* statement, G1a. My single-journey tools cannot reach it. It is also exactly the open part of the Romik–Śniady comparison (z5).
