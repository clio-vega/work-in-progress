**What this session adds to this morning's c1 file:**
1. **Blind replication.** I re-ran the brief without knowing c1 had already done it, and re-derived the same reduction
   (Exchange ⇐ MC+SW). My scripts were separately written, and the numbers match c1's: Exchange 134/134, the shift φ 134/134 (208/448 steps shifted),
   untranslated control 0/134, SW 987/987, row-swap 1426/1426.
2. **The caveat narrows.** Purbhoo §2.3 p.10 says "A single rhombus ♦ ∈ α is accessible if the unique flock on {♦} is accessible".
   So every single move is a Purbhoo migration of an accessible one-rhombus flock. The composites in MC/SW are compositions of his
   migrations, in the same way as the composites in his Cor 3.3. They do not lie "outside his definition". What his paper does not assert is the
   *identities* (MC), (SW).
3. **A correction to c1.** c1's "every B/C detour rejoins (779/779)" measured only the B-journey. A C-journey crossing a B-wake
   rejoins its solo route in only **480/779** cases. In the other 299 it lands *inside* the wake.
4. **Control on local classifiers.** The brief's pre-registered control fails for both per-journey classifiers (first-contact hexagon; full
   crossing word). B/B pairs land in B/C classes. So what one journey sees locally cannot be the content of (MC).
5. **(SW) is a crossing too.** g's swapped journey crosses h's wake for 3–8 steps, then runs exactly the tail of h's solo route. On the B side the
   prefix is longer by exactly one. A single uniform crossing lemma would give both (MC) and (SW).
   The crossed window word is the *reverse* of the solo word in about 82% of cases. The exceptions trade a↔bb, so a crossing lemma stated
   at the level of type words would be false.
6. **Wider test** with a different sampler (seeded random walks; gl5; |λ|,|μ| ≤ 6). MC 2994/2994, SW 1029/1029, row-swap 1543/1543. The B/B control commutes in only 753/1497, so the test can see failures. 312 cells, |α| ≤ 5 (stopped by hand).
