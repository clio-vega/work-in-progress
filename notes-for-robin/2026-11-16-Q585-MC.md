**Status, unchanged in kind.** (MC) and (SW) are still *computed*. G1 is still *conditional on (MC)+(SW)*, and H1 is unchanged.

**What is new**
1. **Shelf (step 0): null over 1209.1262 and 1401.1725.** I grepped both e-prints first-hand. Neither has a lemma saying that moves in different directions
   commute. BKPT's remark after `lem:chain` says propagation paths *cross* and that the order matters, in the two-step setting. So I am no
   longer asking you for a "gashes commute" lemma from those two papers.
2. **Wake Crossing Lemma (Purbhoo pp.17–18), read first-hand.** It is a *same-direction* lemma: the second rhombus does not cross the
   midline. That is the B/B riding behaviour. In (MC) the second journey runs at 150° and does cross. So the hypothesis that fails for (MC) is
   "runs alongside the wake".
3. **Proved: a hexagon lemma, giving triangle conservation.** Lift every tile to Λ₀×Λ₁ ≅ Z⁴, the R⁴ lift. A degree/area argument shows that every
   180° migration step is a unit lozenge of two same-type triangles plus a unit segment of the other lattice, and that the step slides the lozenge
   along that segment. Every 120° step is Δ₀+Δ₁. As a corollary, the triangle *shapes* never change, so a state's triangles are two functions
   q: {0-triangles} → Λ₁ and p: {1-triangles} → Λ₀. Two planar-topology steps are argued but not formalised; this is recorded in the tex.
4. **Computed: every migration is a unit push.** Within one journey each triangle moves at most once. A B-move lowers one barycentric
   coordinate of q by 1, and a C-move raises another by 1. So each migration adds 1 to a linear height on a set of triangles (its wake).
5. **Computed: (MC) becomes a union law, and the B/B control kills it.** Census, cap 60, the same diamonds as c1/c2. The moved sets
   differ between the two orders, but their union does not: 1558/1558 (B/C) and 987/987 (SW). On B/B the union law holds in exactly
   the 339/779 commuting pairs and fails in all 440 others. The coverage includes c2's 299 land-inside-the-wake cases.
6. **Computed: refined rigidity.** "Nest data determine the state within an orbit" is *false* in 2 of 5192 classes (both assignment swaps).
   "Nest data + the set of cells sent to B determine the state" holds in 9982/9982 classes, 3388 of them reached by several move words.
   If that is true, the tiling half of (MC) follows from the landing half. (SW) is not covered by it.
