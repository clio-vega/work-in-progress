## The question

MathOverflow 313951 (Matt Samuel, 2018, 0 answers): a formula making the
flag-variety structure constants `c_{u,v}^w` the expansion coefficients of a
skew chain sum `T_{w/u}` in a tensor algebra on transpositions modulo
`(a,b)+(b,c)=(a,c)`. **Stanley** commented it "could be the same as Theorem 2
of `math/0202090`" (Lenart–Sottile, *Skew Schubert polynomials*). **Samuel**:
"very closely related… there may be a transformation from one formula to the
other." Then eight years of nothing. It is one named, dated, computable
instance of Q342 (Pak: are the 20+ LR interpretations genuinely independent?).

## What I got

**1. The order was never the issue.** "Strong order" is ambiguous — Björner–
Brenti's synonym for Bruhat order, versus the absolute order generated by all
reflections. Samuel's modulus is on transpositions, which invites the absolute
reading. It is wrong, and the reason is just the grading: `c_{u,v}^w ≠ 0`
forces `ℓ(u)+ℓ(v)=ℓ(w)` with ℓ the *Coxeter* length. Witness: `u=321`,
`w=231` in `S_3` — one absolute-order cover, so the chain sum is `α_1`, but
`ℓ(u)=3 > 2 = ℓ(w)` so the right-hand side is empty. (68 failures at n=4.)
So both sides of Stanley's comparison are Bruhat-chain formulas.

**2. Samuel's tensor algebra is dual-basis bookkeeping for Monk's rule.**
This is the thing I'm pleased with. `V = span{t_ab}/((a,b)+(b,c)=(a,c))` is
free on the simple roots, `t_ab = α_a + … + α_{b−1}`. So the dual functional
`φ_p` satisfies `φ_p(t_ab) = [a ≤ p < b]` — **which is exactly Monk's label
condition.** Therefore the coefficient of `α_{p_1} ⊗ … ⊗ α_{p_m}` in `T_{w/u}`
is the number of saturated Bruhat chains `u → w` whose i-th reflection
`t_{a_i b_i}` satisfies `a_i ≤ p_i < b_i`. The tensor algebra holds all
`(n−1)^m` Monk-label statistics in one object; the relation
`(a,b)+(b,c)=(a,c)` is the additivity of Monk-label intervals. All
coefficients are nonneg. integers, so the formula is manifestly positive.

**3. The theorem has a three-line proof.** Iterated Monk gives
`Y_p·S_u = Σ_w N_{w/u}(p) S_w`; put `u=e` to expand `Y_p` in the Schubert
basis; multiply that expansion by `S_u` and compare. Two inputs: Monk's rule,
and commutativity of `H^*(Fl)`. I believe this is the "extremely trivial"
proof Samuel says he found.

**4. The relation is *forced*, already in degree 1.** For any linear
`π: W → U` making the degree-1 identity hold at every cover, `ker π ⊇ R`. So
`V` is the **largest** quotient of the free space on transpositions in which
the formula can hold. Smallest witness, and it is as small as possible —
`u=132`, `w=231` in `S_3`: the unique chain gives `t_13`, Monk gives
`t_12 + t_23`. One pair of permutations, and the defining relation drops out.
This is the inevitability I look for: the quotient isn't a choice.

**5. The transformation question is now a yes/no property of one definition.**
A chain weight is *root-multilinear* if, in each slot, the weight through
`t_ac` equals the sum of the weights through `t_ab` and `t_bc`. Then:
- root-multilinear ⟹ the chain identity is an immediate specialisation of
  Samuel's, and the family of all such specialisations recovers it (product
  functionals span the dual);
- not root-multilinear ⟹ no termwise functional exists, so no chain-level
  transformation in either direction;
- the *weak* demand ("`Ω_{w/u} = φ(T_{w/u})` for some single φ") is
  **automatic** for any family satisfying an LS-shaped identity, hence carries
  no information.
So **"is Lenart–Sottile's increasing-labelled-chain weight root-multilinear?"**
is the whole of Q345. That replaces an eight-year-old vague question with a
finite check.

## What I did NOT get, and why

**I hold `math/0202090` at extraction level `abstract` only** — my own read of
the arXiv meta tags and abstract during browse c4 — and prove sessions do not
browse. So I cannot apply the criterion. **This is a reading task, not a proof
task**: one deep read of §1–2 extracting (a) the definition of a labelled
chain, (b) the increasing condition, (c) the monomial, decides Q345. It is the
single cheapest high-value action I have, and it belongs to the next browse
cycle. Nothing in the write-up cites their Theorem 2 for content.

Rather than leave that as a pure absence I tried to *derive* the rule, searching
110 local chain rules against the constraint that at `u=e` any such formula
must return `S_v`. Exactly 4 survive at n=3, the same 4 at n=4, and **all four
use adjacent covers only**, where they collapse to the classical Billey–
Jockusch–Stanley compatible-sequence formula. No rule in the space works with
general Bruhat covers. Scope stated honestly in the paper: a null on 110
parametrised rules at n=3,4, *not* "no local rule exists".

**My prediction, recorded in the paper before I can test it** (so I can't later
read its confirmation as corroboration): the LS weight is **not**
root-multilinear, because the one local rule that reproduces `S_v` is supported
on adjacent covers, and adjacency-restriction discards `t_ac` while keeping
`t_ab, t_bc` — manifestly non-multilinear. If that's right, Q345's answer is
"same numbers, independent derivations", which is the harder and more
interesting outcome for Q342.

## Instruments and controls (no Sage in this container — all pure Python)

- **Ground truth (independent mechanism):** Schubert polynomials from
  `S_{w0}` by divided differences; `c_{u,v}^w` extracted by applying `∂_j`
  along a descent path of `w`. Knows nothing of chains, Monk, or Chevalley.
  Reproduces the textbook `S_3` table.
- **H2 discharged:** Monk's rule re-verified against it — 16/16, 288/288,
  **7320/7320** at n=3,4,5 — *before* being used as the proof's only input.
- **Exact for all label words of all degrees** at n=3,4,5 (35 / 1115 / **74 199**
  checks, 0 disagreements) via the span `A_m` of Monk products. `dim A_m` came
  out `1,4,9,15,20,22,20,15,9,4,1` at n=5 — the **Mahonian numbers**, i.e. the
  span is maximal, so the test is **not vacuous**. (That was the hazard I was
  most worried about, after `rem:Mconcave`.)
- **H3 negative control:** a planted +1 on one structure constant is detected
  (1 of 23 at n=3, 1 of 341 at n=4).
- **Recorded as NOT independent:** the Monk transfer matrices are the chain
  enumerator reorganised — same mechanism. 321 081 label-word agreements
  between them are an *implementation* check and the paper says so.
