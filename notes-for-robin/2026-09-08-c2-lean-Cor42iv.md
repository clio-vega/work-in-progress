## What I actually learned

The printed proof ends "this is not identically zero." Four monomials in two variables — it
*looks* like a symbol count, and I wrote it as one. It is not. Formalising forced me to
enumerate how the leading monomial `t^{P-k} s^Q` could be cancelled, and there are three
ways, not two. The third needs `P = Q` **and** `Q + k = P - k`, hence `2k = 0`. It is
excluded by neither of the two hypotheses on its own — only by combining them. That third
case is precisely the seam the printed argument walked past, and it is why the corollary
came out with the wrong quantifier rather than merely a sloppy one.

The `e ≤ 2` collapse then has two genuinely different mechanisms. At `e = 1` the window
`(c, c+1)` contains no integer, so the crossing index is zero by emptiness. At `e = 2` a
nonzero crossing index *pins the geometry* — `k = 1` forces `b = c - 1` — and legality does
the rest, evaluating both single-cell windows to the constants 1 and 0. I had been thinking
of these as one phenomenon ("the range `[0, e-1]` is too small"). They are not; one is a
vacuum and the other is a rigidity.

And Lean took a hypothesis away from me. I stated the collapse with the paper's `x ≠ y` and
the unused-variable linter flagged it: the `k = +1` and `k = -1` windows already force
opposite orders on `x` and `y`, so the case split is exhaustive without it. The lemma is
strictly stronger than I wrote it.

## What is not proved

The bridge from `twoBeadSector` (the honest sum over routes) to a sum of two-assignment
expressions is Theorem 4.1(2)'s enumeration of legal assignments, and that is still
unformalised — it was already out of scope for the 8 September `ts = 1` session. So this is
Cor. 4.2(iv) corrected *modulo* that enumeration, not end-to-end from the sector definition.
The gap is inherited, not new, but it is real and I would rather name it than let
"lean-verified" imply more than it does.

Rick's identification of my window statistic with the `e`-ribbon leg length is recorded in
the module docstring and used nowhere. An off-by-one in that convention would land squarely
inside the range `[0, e-1]` that the whole corollary is built on, and I have not checked it
against a primary source.
