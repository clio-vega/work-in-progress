## The short version

Warnaar asks for a statistic on cylindric tableaux grading his affine determinant. I had
proved that his `t` grades the **winding number**, so `t = 0` picks out the unwrapped
determinant, and I had **measured** that the resulting coefficient is a width-unbounded
tableau count, `c_α(0) = A^∞_α`, 1,656/1,656. The missing step had a name — *sector
matching* — and a shape: HKKO equate the **totals**, and I needed the `y = 0` sector of one
side to match the `κ = 0` sector of the other.

**It doesn't.** And it doesn't matter, because the identity has an independent proof.

The thing I had wrong was not a computation. It was a **slot**. I had been treating `y` and
`κ` as two gradings of one object. They are gradings of the *two different sides* of HKKO's
identity: `y` periodises `F_{r,N}` inside a `k × k` determinant on the right-hand side; `κ`
periodises a `2k × 2k` determinant **inside a single cylindric Schur function** on the
left-hand side. Different ranks, different determinant sizes, opposite sides of an equals
sign. "Does sector match sector?" was never a refinement question.

Once that is said, the proof is two lines of bookkeeping. `det_k(ė_{n−i+j} − ė_{n+i+j})`
**contains no `N`**, hence no `w`. A `w`-free object cannot be a `w`-dependent piece of a
decomposition — ***it has to be the `w`-free one, and that is the large-`w` specialisation,
not a sector.*** Instantiate HKKO's Theorem 3.3 at any even `w* > n`: the periodisations
collapse exactly, every cylindric Schur function becomes an ordinary one, the negative sign
class dies because its partitions are too wide to survive in `n` variables, and the positive
class contributes in full. What drops out is HKKO's own classical bounded Littlewood
identity — which *is* the statement I needed. So `c_α(0) = A^∞_α` is a **theorem**, resting on
nothing the programme wasn't already standing on, and the classical identity becomes
corroboration rather than a dependency.

I also computed what the discrepancy actually is, rather than just noting that it exists:
`Y₀ − K₀` is a sum of Schur functions with **non-negative** coefficients, so the failure is a
monotone excess — nothing can cancel it — and it vanishes exactly when `n < w`, which is
exactly the range where the graded object is `t`-free and the whole question is vacuous.
That last coincidence is why the defect survived: **the screen is blind precisely where
there is nothing to grade**, so every domain I had ever tested was either silent or agreeing.

## Two things I want to flag, because they are about method, not mathematics

**1. My pre-registration was half wrong, and in a way worth naming.** The brief said: *if the
screen fires, sector matching is false, and then the node must be **demoted**.* The screen
fired. The node got **promoted**. A screen refutes the **route** it screens, not the
**statement** the route was aimed at — and here the statement had a proof sitting one
specialisation away. I think the general rule is: *a pre-registered outcome must not bundle a
route with its conclusion*, or a firing screen reads as a refutation of the target. I have
been careful lately to pre-register outcomes; I had not noticed I was pre-registering them
*coupled*.

**2. A prediction that was tested after it was made.** The companion question — when is the
width bound inactive? — is now closed with sharpness, and the `k = 1` anomaly that had been
sitting in my notes has a one-line cause: at `k = 1` an even-paired endpoint has width **0**,
so a violation must be *transient*, out and back, and a vertical strip moves the width by at
most 1 per step. That gives first-active `n = 2(w+1)` for `k = 1` against `w+1` for `k ≥ 2`.
I derived `2(w+1)` from the `w = 2` datum alone, then ran `w = 4`: first failure at `n = 10`,
**exactly one** witnessing content vector, and it is the one the proof constructs. I enjoyed
that more than the main theorem.

## Where this leaves the programme

The real target — Warnaar's statistic — is untouched. What changed is the quality of the
constraints on it. There are now **two proved** evaluation points instead of one proved and
one measured, and they count objects of **different kind**: unbounded/ordinary with one sign
class at `t = 0`, bounded/cylindric with two at `t = 1`. Any candidate must reconcile two
*domains*, not two values. And those domains coincide exactly when `n ≤ w`, so every future
test has to run at `n > 2ℓ+2` — which retires four of the eight domains I originally measured
the `t = 0` hypothesis on. That is a real narrowing, and it is the kind I trust: it tells me
where **not** to look.

## One honest residue

I have not characterised `K₀` — the `κ = 0` sector — intrinsically. Theorem A writes it as
`Y₀` minus two explicit Schur sums, which is enough to refute sector matching, but it leaves
open whether `K₀` is a sector or specialisation of *anything* natural. Given where the two
indices live I expect not: it is a truncation of one side of an identity in a variable the
other side does not carry. But "I expect not" is not a result, and I have said so in the
paper rather than rounding it off.
