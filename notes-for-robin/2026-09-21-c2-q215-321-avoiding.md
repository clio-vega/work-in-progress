## The short version

For 321-avoiding $w \in \widetilde S_n$ and every $r$:
$\operatorname{supp}\widetilde F_w(x_1,\dots,x_r) = \mathcal P_{\mu(w)} \cap \mathbb Z^r$,
M-convex, saturated Newton polytope. **Consuming Lam 2006 only — not Lam 2008 Cor 8.5.**

**The conclusions are not new; the route is.** WZZ already prove M-convexity for every $w$,
and WZZ + Lam's monomial dominance already give the polytope for every $w$. What their proof
consumes, and what the paragraph above their Problems 5.1–5.2 objects to, is dual $k$-Schur
positivity. I avoid it on the 321-avoiding class. I am also explicit in §9 that this is *not*
a solution of Problem 5.1: its text asks for a proof from the decomposition definition, and I
swap one import for another.

I mention this because yesterday's ledger and this morning's brief had both drifted into
claiming the theorem. My own 09-20 abstract had the framing right; the drift took fifteen
hours. The browse phase caught it at source before anything went out.

## What I would most like you to look at

**1. Whether I am right that gap (iii) of my 09-20 paper was simply false.** That gap said the
greedy $\hat\lambda$ "grows with $\ell$ until $\ell$ reaches the minimal chain length, after
which it is constant", and filed it cosmetic. The recursion
$g^t_i = \min(g^{t-1}_{i+1}-1,\lambda_i)$ has no $\ell$ in it at all. So below $\ell_{\min}$
the polynomial is zero and $\hat\lambda$ is undefined; at and above, it is constant. There is
no growth regime and I have withdrawn the sentence. It is a two-line lemma, which is exactly
why I distrust my having got it wrong the first time.

**2. A sentence in Lam's proof I cannot follow** (`math/0501335`, l. 1868). In the branch where
the letter $i$ is absent from the reduced word, the base shape changes $\mu \to \lambda$ and
the text then says "it is clear that $u_w\cdot\lambda \ne 0$" — while the inductive hypothesis
was about $\mu$. I have confirmed the sentence says this. **I have not confirmed it is a gap**;
it may be clear and I may be missing it. My whole theorem rests on that proposition, so if you
or Rick can read that paragraph I would be grateful. Empirically the conclusion is fine: 637
of 637 321-avoiding $w$ admit a realisation and 708 of 708 non-avoiding ones do not, over
$n=3,\dots,6$.

**3. A wording slip in the same paper worth knowing about.** Lam defines 321-avoiding as "no
reduced word contains a *subsequence* $i\,(i{+}1)\,i$". Read literally that is strictly stronger
than his own pattern criterion — $w=(-3,4,5)\in\widetilde S_3$, unique reduced word
$s_0s_1s_2s_0$, has the subsequence but no braid factor and no 321 pattern. His own proof shows
the factor is meant. Harmless once seen; I lost twenty minutes to it.

## The sharp small result

Yesterday's paper abandoned the route "the prefix set $A_t$ has a maximum in the right weak
order" because it fails in 178 of 767 triples. Splitting those 178 by 321-avoidance gives
**zero**. All 410 triples with 321-avoiding $w$ have a maximum. So the abandoned route works
exactly where the cylindric greedy chain lives. I have *not* claimed the two are equivalent —
(H4) is about maxima of lengths, this is about maxima in the weak order, and the second is
strictly stronger — but "does 321-avoidance characterise it?" is the question I would take
into the next prove session.

Also fallen out: **(H4) is now proved on the 321-avoiding class**, which was the single
remaining gap of the 09-21-c1 paper; and the greedy cylindric chain turns out to compute the
conjugate of the sorted code of $w^{-1}$, an identity between two old objects that I have not
seen stated.
