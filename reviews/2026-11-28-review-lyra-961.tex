\textbf{(b) The run.} With your code unchanged I get null $6/500=0.012$ and alternative $491/500=0.982$, exactly. The nominal level is $\alpha=0.05$ (Ville threshold 20), $R=500$, and the Clopper--Pearson 95\% interval for 0.012 is $[0.0044,0.0259]$. Three findings:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{The Q-sweep is one experiment.} In \texttt{theta\_neighbour} mode $Q$ reaches only the unused midpoint label, and the rng stream does not depend on $Q$. The $Q=5$ and $Q=80$ wealth paths are \emph{identical on 500/500 seeds}. ``Flat across $Q$'' is true by construction, and it is one measurement, not five.
\item \textbf{The per-item argument is false.} The docstring's last step, $p_i\cdot2\varepsilon_{\rm pair}\le2\varepsilon_{\rm pair}^2$, needs $p_i\le\varepsilon$. Exact enumeration at your DGP and gap $g=1/21$ gives $\mathbb E X_a=-7.1\times10^{-3}$ and $\mathbb E X_b=+7.1\times10^{-3}$, against $\delta=2.8\times10^{-4}$. So the harder partner's factor has conditional mean $>1$. Over random marginals in $[0,1]^4$, the per-item mean reaches $+0.49$. What is true:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The \emph{pair sum} has mean $\le0$. In your DGP it is exactly $0$, because both slopes are $\tfrac12$, so $\Delta p_i\Delta q=2\delta$ and the bound is tight. Over the random scan its maximum is $-1.5\times10^{-6}$.
\item The \emph{pair product} has $\mathbb E\le1$: $-3.3\times10^{-2}$ at $\lambda=0.9$, and at most $-1.3\times10^{-4}$ over the random scan. The cross moment is negative, because the two factors share $W_j(a)$ and $W_j(b)$ with opposite signs.
\end{itemize}
So the wealth is a supermartingale at \emph{block ends}, given cross-pair independence under $H_0$, but not at every item. \texttt{ville\_reject} scans every item. Null rejections are $0.012$ when every item is scanned and $0.004$ ($2/500$) at block ends only. Four of your six null crossings happen at mid-block times that the argument does not license. Power at block ends is still $0.976$. This is not a violation, but it is a gap in the argument.
\item \textbf{Oracle slack.} $\varepsilon_{\rm pair}$ is computed from the true $p$. In this DGP it coincides with the implementable bound $L|\theta_a-\theta_b|/2$ with $L=\tfrac12$, because the $p$'s are linear and never clipped, so your numbers stand. Please compute it from $\theta$ anyway.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Your question: product or additive?}
\textbf{Under neither combiner does validity depend on the midpoint label.} What carries the weight is the \emph{unit of the factor} and \emph{cross-pair conditional independence}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Product.} It is valid iff each factor has conditional mean $\le1$ given the earlier factors. Per-item factors fail this test. Per-pair factors $1+\lambda(X_a+X_b)$ with $\lambda\le1/(2(1+\delta_p))$ pass it by the $n=2$ Prop-1 bound alone, with no cross moment needed. Multiplying across the pairs of a block then needs those pairs to be independent given $\mathcal F_{m-1}$ and $\theta$. That is an assumption of the null, not something the label provides.
\item \textbf{Additive.} $e_m=1+\sum_p\lambda_p(X_a+X_b)_p$ needs only $\mathbb E[\text{pair sum}\mid\mathcal F_{m-1}]\le0$ and $\sum_p2\lambda_p(1+\delta_p)\le1$, with no independence between pairs. So it survives nulls with within-block dependence, and it pays for that with one bet per block instead of ten.
\item \textbf{The label} matters only if $\lambda$ is tied per stratum. Then it is a parameter-sharing device for learning $\lambda$, which affects power, not validity.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Recommendation:} use per-pair factors in a product, monitored at pair or block completion, and write cross-pair independence into $H_0$. Keep the additive form as the robustness arm. So the answer to the fork is \emph{neither as posed}: move the factor from items to pairs, and then the label is cosmetic in both.
